VALLEYS & RIVERS

“Where mountains become water, and water becomes landscape.”

Between the mountains are the valleys. Through the valleys, water finds its way across the landscape. In Orakzai, the relationship between mountain, water and valley has long shaped where people could settle, cultivate land and move between communities. A river is not only a line on a map. It is part of a geographic system.

Following the mountainous elevation framework established on the preceding page, the territory's hydrological network acts as the connective tissue of human geography. Valleys and watercourses provide the resources necessary for permanent settlement, agricultural productivity, and communal interaction.12

“Hydrological landscape composition illustrating mountain runoff descending into valley river systems.”

The Geographic Hydrological System

The movement of water through Orakzai follows a rigorous geographic logic. Precipitation and melting snow upon the high mountain ridges generate surface runoff. This runoff feeds smaller streams and seasonal tributaries, which converge into major watercourses like the Mastura River and Khanki Toi, carving out fertile valley corridors where human settlement and agriculture take root.23

“Conceptual hydrological model linking mountain runoff to valley settlement.”

Orakzai's Major Valleys

Official administrative and geographical records identify three primary valley systems within the territory: the **Khanki Valley**, the **Mastura Valley**, and the **Khurmana Valley**. Each valley acts as a distinct geographic basin, supporting local agricultural production and providing foundational territory for major Orakzai tribal sections.12

Khanki Valley & Khanki Toi

The **Khanki Valley** is traversed by the **Khanki Toi**, a major watercourse originating near Mount Mitta in the western highlands. In regional nomenclature, *Toi* denotes a stream or river channel. The Khanki Toi flows eastward, eventually serving as a primary source feeder for the Kohat Toi system. The valley floor provides vital agricultural land flanked by the Samana Range.13

“Cross-section model of the Khanki Valley flanked by high mountain ridges.”

Mastura Valley & River

The **Mastura Valley** represents the heartland of central Orakzai. Fed by the **Mastura River**—which also originates near the high western peaks—the valley is characterized by wide alluvial terraces and small agricultural plains on both riverbanks. The Mastura River ultimately flows eastward to join the Bara River system, linking Orakzai hydrology directly with the Peshawar basin drainage.24

“Flow sequence from western headwaters through the Mastura Valley to the Bara River.”

Khurmana Valley & Seasonal Watercourses

The **Khurmana Valley** forms a rugged western corridor near the Kurram border, watered by the Khurmana stream system which flows toward the Kurram River. Across the wider territory, particularly in the southwest, numerous dry watercourses and seasonal streams remain dry for much of the year, carrying sudden runoff only during heavy rainfall events.13

“Hydrological model of seasonal watercourses and intermittent rainfall runoff.”

Water, Settlement & Agriculture

In a mountainous region, water availability dictates the distribution of human habitation and arable farming. Approximately 11.5% of Orakzai land is cultivated, concentrated primarily along the valley floors where perennial streams and valley-floor soils permit irrigation for wheat, maize, and fruit orchards.24

AUTHOR'S REFLECTION

The valleys of Orakzai are where history found a place to rest. While the mountains gave us defense and shelter, it was the Mastura and the Khanki Toi that gave us life. Along their banks, our villages grew, our fields were planted, and our communities flourished. A valley is more than fertile ground; it is the physical home where the Orakzai story is nourished every single day.

“The mountains may shape our horizon, but the rivers shape our hearths.”
