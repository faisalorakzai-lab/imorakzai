THE ROLE OF WOMEN

“Women carry knowledge, work, memory and the future of a community.”

A culture is never carried by one generation alone. It is carried through families. Through language. Through food. Through stories. Through work. Through memory. And across all of these spaces, women have always been part of the social history of Orakzai communities. Women may be daughters, mothers, sisters, wives, grandmothers, teachers, farmers, professionals, caregivers, entrepreneurs, storytellers and keepers of family memory. But there is no single experience called ‘the Orakzai woman.’ A woman’s life can be shaped by her village, family, education, economic circumstances, generation, migration history and place of residence. Some traditions are old. Some have changed. Some are family practices rather than community-wide customs. And some differences remain poorly documented. Understanding the role of women therefore requires more than describing tradition. It requires listening to women themselves.

“A multigenerational model of Orakzai female identity and social contribution.”

Defining the Role

A "role" in Orakzai society is not a fixed rule but a dynamic set of responsibilities, activities, and expectations. It encompasses family position, economic contribution, and community participation. While traditional structures remain important, contemporary education and technology are expanding these roles, allowing women to contribute as entrepreneurs, professionals, and digital citizens while remaining anchors of their family heritage.

“The multifaceted dimensions of women's social and economic roles.”

Women and Conflict Prevention

In the Orakzai district, women have played a documented but often invisible role in community safety. Research by Noreen Naseer (2026) identifies tribal women as essential "early warning" sensors. By observing changes in community dynamics and identifying tensions within the household and neighborhood, they issue warnings to family elders, effectively serving as a frontline for conflict prevention. This agency demonstrates that women's roles extend far beyond the domestic sphere into the protection of the community's future.1

“The pathway of women's agency in community safety and security.”

Agriculture, Food Security, and Economy

Women are the backbone of the rural economy in Orakzai. They are essential to the agricultural workforce, managing livestock, processing food, and maintaining household gardens. Research by M. Safdar (2021) highlights that while their contributions are vital for food security, they often lack formal control over farming resources. In the contemporary context, women are also exploring entrepreneurship through home-based production, crafts, and increasingly, digital commerce.5

Agricultural Knowledge

Managing seeds, livestock health, and seasonal crop processing for family survival.

Economic Agency

Transforming skills into income through textiles, food products, and online trade.

Education and Generational Change

Education is a major driver of social change for Orakzai women. While literacy rates in the district remain below 30% (2023 Census), the push for female education is growing. Education provides not just literacy, but agency—the ability to navigate professional life, access health information, and contribute to community decision-making. Each generation of women reinterprets tradition, blending ancestral wisdom with modern opportunity.4

“The impact of education on female agency and community progress.”

Technology and Digital Future

Technology is creating new bridges for Orakzai women. Smartphones and digital platforms allow for remote education, commerce, and communication within the diaspora. AI assistance is emerging as a tool for translation, research, and digital preservation of oral histories. However, this progress must be navigated responsibly, ensuring that technology amplifies women's voices without replacing their original testimony or violating their privacy.

“The responsible integration of technology in preserving women's heritage.”

AUTHOR'S REFLECTION

A culture is never carried by one generation alone, and it is never carried by one gender alone. In Orakzai, women have always been the quiet architects of our resilience. They are the ones who remember the lullabies that teach us our language, the recipes that connect us to our land, and the stories that define our families. But their role is not just to look backward. Today, Orakzai women are teachers, doctors, farmers, and entrepreneurs who are building the future of our district. To understand Orakzai is to recognize the dignity, strength, and agency of its women. We must listen to their voices, for in their memory and their work, we find the true continuity of our people.

“THE VOICE IS THE ARCHIVE OF THE HEART.”
