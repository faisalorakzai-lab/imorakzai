ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE

“Machines that learn from data, reason over information, and generate content.”

“Artificial intelligence is one of the oldest ambitions in computing: Can a machine perform tasks that appear to require intelligence? For decades, the question belonged mostly to laboratories. Researchers built systems that played games, solved mathematical problems, recognized patterns and processed language. Then the scale changed. More data became available. Computing power increased. Neural-network methods improved. Specialized hardware accelerated training. And software systems began producing text, images, audio, video and code at a scale that changed the public understanding of AI. Artificial intelligence is no longer only a research subject. It is becoming part of the infrastructure of modern life. But AI is not magic. It is mathematics, algorithms, data, computation, engineering and human design. Understanding that distinction is essential. Because the future of AI will not be determined only by how powerful models become. It will also be determined by how responsibly people build and use them.”

Technical Foundations: From ML to Transformers

Modern AI is primarily driven by Machine Learning (ML), where systems learn patterns from data rather than being explicitly programmed. Deep Learning, a subset of ML using multi-layer neural networks, has enabled breakthroughs in vision, speech, and language. The Transformer architecture, introduced in 2017, revolutionized the field by using Attention mechanisms to process sequences of data (like text) in parallel, leading to the rise of Large Language Models (LLMs) and Foundation Models.

These models are trained on massive datasets to predict the next token in a sequence, allowing them to generate fluent text, code, and multimodal content. However, they are probabilistic systems and can produce plausible but incorrect information, a phenomenon known as hallucination. Techniques like Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) and human-in-the-loop evaluation are used to improve reliability and grounding.

AI in Pakistan: Policy & Research (2025–2026)

Pakistan's AI landscape is guided by the National AI Policy 2025, which aims to transform the country into a knowledge-based economy. The National Center of Artificial Intelligence (NCAI), with labs in six major universities, serves as the institutional anchor, having developed over 220 AI products for sectors such as healthcare, agriculture, and energy. Research is also focused on Pashto AI, addressing the challenges of low-resource language processing through OCR and speech recognition initiatives.

Logic Atlas: Artificial Intelligence

Risks, Safety & Governance

The rapid advancement of AI introduces significant risks, including algorithmic bias, privacy leakage, and security vulnerabilities like prompt injection. Governance frameworks, such as the NIST AI Risk Management Framework, provide standards for identifying and mitigating these risks. As AI systems become more autonomous through AI Agents, the challenge of Alignment—ensuring AI behavior remains consistent with human values—becomes a central research and policy question.
