# Research Notes: Page 121 — Artificial Intelligence

This document records the research findings and methodological decisions for **Page 121: Artificial Intelligence**.

## 1. Technical Foundations
- **Hierarchy:** AI (Broad field) > Machine Learning (Patterns from data) > Deep Learning (Neural networks) > Generative AI (Content generation).
- **Architecture:** The **Transformer** model (Attention mechanism) is the primary driver of current large-scale language and multimodal systems.
- **Training vs. Inference:** Distinguishing between the compute-heavy training phase and the deployment phase (inference), which is increasingly moving to the edge.

## 2. Modern AI Capabilities
- **Multimodal Systems:** Integration of text, image, audio, and video processing into single foundation models.
- **Agentic AI:** Transition from static chatbots to autonomous agents that can use tools (APIs, search, code execution) to achieve goals.
- **RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation):** A key architecture for grounding AI outputs in verified external data to reduce hallucinations.

## 3. Pakistan Context (2025–2026)
- **National AI Policy 2025:** Approved July 2025; targets 90% AI awareness by 2026 and training 1 million learners.
- **NCAI (National Center of AI):** Central institutional body with labs in 6 universities (NUST, NED, UET, etc.); has developed 220+ AI products for sectors like healthcare, agriculture, and smart cities.
- **Pashto AI:** Research focus on OCR for Pashto script, speech-to-text, and translation, addressing the challenge of low-resource language datasets.

## 4. Case Study: Faisal Orakzai — AI & Digital Systems
- **Perspective:** Viewing AI as a tool for problem-solving and system optimization rather than a replacement for human agency.
- **Integration:** Interest in the intersection of AI, blockchain (for provenance/identity), and digital infrastructure.
- **Role:** Illustrates the "Builder's Path"—moving from learning fundamentals to building integrated digital products.

## 5. Risks & Governance
- **Reliability:** Addressing hallucinations and the "Capability vs. Reliability" gap.
- **Safety & Ethics:** Bias in training data, privacy concerns, and the concentration of compute power.
- **Governance:** Following the **NIST AI Risk Management Framework** as a technical standard for responsible deployment.

## 6. Structural Challenges
- **Compute Gap:** Access to high-end GPUs for local researchers and startups remains a constraint.
- **Data Provenance:** Ensuring ethical sourcing and ownership of data used for training local models.
- **Education:** The need for deep technical skills (math, systems) beyond simple prompt engineering.
