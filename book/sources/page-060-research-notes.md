# Research Notes: Page 60 — Traditional Food

## Core Concept
- **Food as Identity**: In Orakzai and Pashtun society, food is a nexus of land, agriculture, family, and hospitality.
- **Evidence-Based Documentation**: Distinguishing between direct Orakzai evidence (maize, wheat, meat) and broader regional or Pashtun-wide culinary traditions.

## Key Findings

### 1. Orakzai Staple Foods
- **Maize (Zea mays)**: Directly documented as a primary crop and staple. FAO (2018) distributed certified maize seeds to 3,750 farmer families in Orakzai Agency to restore food security.
- **Wheat (Triticum aestivum)**: Identified as a staple food in historical and official records. Used primarily for bread (Dodai/Naan).
- **Meat**: Historical sources identify meat as a staple food for the Orakzai population, reflecting the livestock-based economy of the region.

### 2. Pashtun Food Culture (Pashtun-Wide / Regional)
- **Commensality**: The practice of eating together from shared platters (Thaal) is a core Pashtun cultural value, reinforcing tribal and family bonds.
- **Hospitality (Melmastia)**: Food is intrinsic to hospitality. The obligation to offer tea and meals to guests is a fundamental part of the Pashtunwali code.
- **Hujra Role**: The Hujra serves as a primary space for communal eating and welcoming guests, particularly for men.

### 3. Agriculture and Food Production
- **Crop Diversity**: Official records document the cultivation of vegetables (potatoes, turnips, spinach) and fruits (apricots, apples, walnuts) in Orakzai.
- **Livestock Connection**: Sheep and goats provide milk, yogurt, butter, and meat, connecting the rural economy directly to the household kitchen.
- **Women's Role**: FAO documents women's active participation in agriculture and food management in Orakzai villages (e.g., Andkhel Bala).

### 4. Modern Adaptation
- **Urbanization**: Migration to cities like Peshawar and Kohat introduces Orakzai families to urban markets and standardized regional dishes (e.g., Chapli Kabab, Pulao).
- **Digital Memory**: Use of social media (WhatsApp, Instagram) for sharing family recipes and documenting festive meals.
- **AI and Heritage**: Potential for AI to transcribe oral histories of food and organize digital family recipe archives.

## Evidence Hierarchy
- **A. Orakzai-Specific**: Maize seed distribution (FAO 2018); Historical staple records (Maize, Wheat, Meat).
- **B. Pashtun-Wide**: Commensality among Durrani and other tribes; Hujra hospitality; Melmastia principles.
- **C. Regional**: KP regional agriculture (wheat, vegetables); regional dishes like Chapli Kabab (labeled regional).
- **D. Islamic**: Halal principles; Ramadan fasting and Iftar; Eid-ul-Adha meat sharing.
- **E. Contemporary**: Digital recipe sharing; urban food adaptation; hospital/modern food systems.
- **F. Oral History**: Family memories of traditional bread-making and seasonal fruit preservation.

## Documented Terminology
- **Dodai**: Pashto for bread.
- **Naan**: Regional term for flatbread.
- **Thaal**: Large shared platter for communal eating.
- **Melmastia**: Hospitality.
- **Qurbani**: Sacrifice (Eid-ul-Adha).
- **Fitrana**: Charity (Eid-ul-Fitr).
