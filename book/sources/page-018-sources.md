# Sources: Page 18 — Melmastia — Hospitality

This document records the academic and historical sources supporting **Page 18: Melmastia — Hospitality** in *I’M ORAKZAI*.

## Primary References & Academic Citations

1. **Encyclopaedia Iranica & Anthropological Scholarship**: *Melmastia as a Customary Social Relationship*.
   - **Authors**: Anthropologists specializing in Pashtun social structures.
   - **Publisher**: Encyclopædia Iranica Foundation, New York.
   - **Details**: Defines melmastia as a foundational customary norm involving the reception, protection, and respect of guests.
   - **Claim Supported**: Melmastia as a social relationship and ethical value.

2. **Akbar S. Ahmed & eHRAF World Cultures (Yale University)**: *Guest-Host Relationships and Shared Food*.
   - **Authors**: Akbar S. Ahmed and comparative ethnographers.
   - **Details**: Examines the cultural significance of hospitality, shared meals (tea, bread), and generosity in tribal societies.
   - **Claim Supported**: Role of shared food and host-guest interaction.

3. **Historical Ethnographies**: *The Hujra and Communal Hospitality*.
   - **Details**: Chronicles the institutional role of the hujra as a guesthouse, meeting place, and center for guest reception in Pashtun communities.
   - **Claim Supported**: Hujra as the physical setting for melmastia.

4. **Sociological Research on Contemporary Pashtun Society**: *Urbanization and Diaspora Adaptation*.
   - **Details**: Documents how traditional hospitality practices adapt to modern urban and diaspora environments (apartments, digital communication) while retaining core cultural values.
   - **Claim Supported**: Modern adaptation of hospitality.
