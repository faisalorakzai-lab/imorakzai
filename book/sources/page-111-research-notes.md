# Research Notes: Page 111 — Pakistan’s Technology Revolution

This document records the research findings and methodological decisions for **Page 111: Pakistan’s Technology Revolution**.

## 1. Historical Evolution and Milestones
- **Early Infrastructure (1947–1990s):** Focus on telegraph, telephone, and postal services. Establishment of PTCL.
- **Computing (1960s–1980s):** Introduction of mainframes in banks and government institutions. University-led computer science education begins.
- **Internet Era (1990s–2010):** Launch of dial-up ISPs, followed by broadband. Pakistan's first software houses established.
- **Mobile Revolution (2004–Present):** Deregulation of the telecom sector. Launch of 3G/4G (2014) as a major turning point for digital adoption.
- **Digital Economy (2018–Present):** Rise of fintech (Raast, mobile wallets), startups, e-commerce, and freelancing.

## 2. Key Statistics (Reporting Period: 2025–2026)
- **Telecom (PTA):** Surpassed **200 million** telecom subscribers and **150 million** broadband subscribers by June 2025. Data usage reached **27,727 petabytes** in 2025.
- **IT Exports (SBP/PSEB):** ICT export remittances reached an all-time high of **US$4.18 billion** during July 2025–May 2026. Monthly exports hit a record **$386 million** in October 2025.
- **Financial Inclusion (SBP):** Raast instant payment system and mobile wallets (Easypaisa, JazzCash) are primary drivers of branchless banking.

## 3. Policy and Emerging Technology
- **National AI Policy (2025):** Framework focusing on AI governance, technical skills, research, and public-sector adoption.
- **Digital Government:** Initiatives like NADRA (digital identity), FBR (tax systems), and provincial e-government portals.
- **Startup Ecosystem:** Growth in fintech, e-commerce, and logistics, supported by incubators and accelerators (Plan9, NIC).

## 4. Orakzai and Technology
- **Connectivity:** Increasing mobile broadband penetration in Orakzai District post-2018 merger.
- **Opportunities:** Youth engagement in remote work, freelancing, and online education.
- **Digital Divide:** Geographic and infrastructure constraints (electricity, network stability) remain significant challenges.

## 5. Evidence Boundaries & Cautions
- **Statistical Precision:** Clearly distinguish between financial years (FY) and calendar years. Use PTA and SBP as primary authoritative sources.
- **No Exaggeration:** Avoid promotional industry language; focus on measurable outcomes and documented constraints.
- **AI Role:** AI is presented as a transition tool for software development and language processing (Pashto), not as a fully mature sector.
