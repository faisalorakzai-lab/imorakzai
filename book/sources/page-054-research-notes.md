# Research Notes: Page 54 — Traditional Clothing

## Core Concept
- **Clothing as Memory**: Garments reveal climate, occupation, and identity.
- **Layered Identity**: Orakzai clothing exists within Pashtun-wide, regional (KP/FATA), and local/family frameworks.

## Key Findings

### 1. Men's Traditional Dress
- **Khet Partug (کټ پرتوګ)**: Traditional outfit consisting of a knee-length tunic (khet) and loose trousers (partug). Widely documented in former FATA regions including Orakzai.
- **Shalwar Kameez**: The standard modern and traditional attire across Pakistan and the Pashtun belt.
- **Turban (Lungee/Pagri)**: Traditional headwear, with variations in wrapping and material indicating regional or tribal affiliation.
- **Pakol (پکول)**: Woolen cap associated with northern Pashtun regions and widely worn in Orakzai.
- **Peshawari Chappal**: Traditional leather footwear, strongly associated with KP identity.
- **Chadar/Shawl**: Used for warmth and modesty, often in earth tones or deep charcoal.

### 2. Women's Traditional Dress
- **Firaq Partug (فراق پرتوګ)**: Traditional colorful tunic and trousers.
- **Embroidery**: Intricate hand-embroidery and waxed cloth designs are documented in Orakzai (Gilmour, 2013).
- **Patterns**: Specific Orakzai dress patterns are examined in contemporary design research (MF Ahmad, 2025, Massey University).
- **Jewelry**: Traditional silver and beadwork common in regional Pashtun culture.

### 3. Orakzai-Specific Evidence
- **Hujra Dress**: Loose khet partug specifically noted in Orakzai social contexts.
- **Alisherzai Variation**: Historical mentions of specific clothing styles among Alisherzai Orakzai in the Kharmana valley.
- **Design Research**: MF Ahmad (2025) identifies basic Orakzai dress patterns in a design revival study.

### 4. Change and Adaptation
- **Generational Shift**: Older generations favor traditional khet partug and turbans; younger generations blend traditional shalwar kameez with modern waistcoats or western elements.
- **Urbanization**: Migration to cities like Kohat and Peshawar leads to more standardized Pakistani styles while retaining Pashtun identity markers.
- **Digital Culture**: Social media and online businesses are reviving traditional patterns for diaspora communities.

## Evidence Hierarchy
- **A. Orakzai-Specific**: Malik/Hujra dress responsibilities; Alisherzai regional styles; MF Ahmad (2025) pattern study.
- **B. Pashtun-Wide**: Melmastia-related dress codes; Turban wrapping; Peshawari Chappal.
- **C. Regional (KP/FATA)**: Khet partug; Firaq partug; specific embroidery techniques.
- **D. Historical**: British-era photographs and gazetteer descriptions of Tirah residents.
