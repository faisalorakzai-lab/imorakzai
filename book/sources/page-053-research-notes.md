# Research Notes: Page 53 — Orakzai Hospitality

## Core Concept
- **Melmastia (مېلمستیا)**: A fundamental pillar of Pashtunwali, commonly translated as hospitality, but encompassing a broader social contract of recognition, respect, and protection.
- **Orakzai Context**: Hospitality is practiced within the framework of family, kinship, and community institutions like the Hujra.

## Key Findings

### 1. Melmastia as a Social Institution
- **Definition**: More than friendliness; it is a social obligation.
- **Components**: Welcoming, generosity, food/refreshments, social recognition, and protection.
- **Dignity**: The guest is received with honor (Nang) and dignity (Ghairat).
- **Source**: *Encyclopaedia Iranica*; Akbar S. Ahmed (1980).

### 2. The Hujra and Hospitality
- **Role**: The primary public space for receiving guests, especially for men.
- **Malik's Responsibility**: In Orakzai villages (e.g., Mirbak), the Malik is expected to maintain a Hujra, which involves significant expense due to hospitality requirements.
- **Source**: *Journal of Social Sciences and Humanities* (Infinity Press).

### 3. Food and Dastarkhan
- **Symbolism**: Sharing a meal (Dastarkhan) creates a social bond.
- **Tea**: A regional/Pashtun-wide staple of hospitality, often the first offering.
- **Evidence**: Broader Pashtun practice; Orakzai practice aligns with regional norms.

### 4. Impact of Security and Modernity
- **Militancy**: Ongoing insecurity in former FATA (including Orakzai) has constrained the traditional ideal of welcoming unknown strangers.
- **Urbanization/Migration**: Hospitality adapts in cities like Kohat, Peshawar, and Karachi, shifting from Hujra-based to home or community association-based gatherings.
- **Source**: Ullah (2019), "Militancy and Pashtun Culture."

### 5. Reciprocity and Economy
- **Social Exchange**: Hospitality is a non-commercial exchange, though it may involve social reciprocity (future host/guest roles).
- **Religious Intersection**: Islamic ethics of welcoming guests complement local Pashtun norms.

## Evidence Scale for Page 53
- **Orakzai-Specific**: Hujra responsibilities of Maliks in Orakzai villages.
- **Regional/Pashtun**: General Melmastia principles, Dastarkhan, and tea service.
- **Modern Adaptations**: Impact of militancy on social fabric in KP/former FATA.
