# Sources: Page 38 — Traditional Orakzai Governance

This document records the academic and historical sources supporting **Page 37: Traditional Orakzai Governance** in *I’M ORAKZAI*.

## Primary References & Academic Citations

1. **King, Lucas White**: *Monograph on the Orakzai Country and Clans*.
   - **Publication**: Punjab Government Press, 1900.
   - **Details**: Provides detailed observations on the decentralized social organization of Orakzai sections, the role of customary practices (Rewaj), and the interactions between tribal authority and the Punjab government.
   - **Claim Supported**: Decentralized nature of Orakzai governance; role of Rewaj and kinship in social order.

2. **Khan, Sarfraz and Noor Ul Amin**: *Demystifying Tribal Autonomous Governance: A Critical Appraisal of Maliki System in Federally Administered Tribal Areas (FATA)*.
   - **Publication**: *Central Asia*, Vol. 76, No. 1, 2015.
   - **Details**: Academic analysis of how the Maliki system evolved from traditional social authority into a state-recognized intermediary role during the colonial and post-colonial periods.
   - **Claim Supported**: Evolution of the Maliki system; distinction between customary authority and state-recognized Maliki.

3. **Akhtar, Z.**: *ADR (Grand Jirga), Truth, Justice and Reconciliation in the Tribal Areas*.
   - **Publication**: Queen Mary University of London, Law and Human Rights Docs.
   - **Details**: Discusses the relationship between Pashtunwali (the ideal code) and Rewaj (local custom) in tribal governance and conflict resolution.
   - **Claim Supported**: Rewaj as a foundation of customary order; interaction between Pashtunwali and local governance.

4. **Shah, K. M.**: *Too little, too late: The mainstreaming of Pakistan's tribal regions*.
   - **Publication**: *Observer Research Foundation (ORF)*, 2019.
   - **Details**: Analyzes the historical administrative structure of the FATA, the role of the Frontier Crimes Regulation (FCR), and the 2018 constitutional merger.
   - **Claim Supported**: Historical administrative structure of FATA; the 2018 transition to modern constitutional governance.

5. **Sammon, Robert L.**: *Mullas and Maliks: Evolution of the FATA and the Maliki System*.
   - **Publication**: Lauder Institute, University of Pennsylvania, 2008.
   - **Details**: Examines the dual power structures of religious figures and tribal intermediaries in the tribal areas.
   - **Claim Supported**: Overlap of religious and tribal influence in governance.

## Research Notes on Governance Frameworks

- **Governance vs. Government**: Governance is the broader social organization of collective life; government refers to formal state institutions.
- **Decentralized Order**: Authority in traditional Orakzai society was distributed across sections, clans, and families rather than concentrated in a central office.
- **Rewaj (Customary Practice)**: A flexible, memory-based system of norms accepted by the community, distinct from a written legal code.
- **Limitations**: Traditional governance was historically male-dominated and lacked modern procedural safeguards for vulnerable individuals.
