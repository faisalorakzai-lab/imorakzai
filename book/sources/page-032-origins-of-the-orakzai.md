# Sources: Page 32 — Origins of the Orakzai

This document records the academic and historical sources supporting **Page 32: Origins of the Orakzai** in *I’M ORAKZAI*.

## Primary References & Academic Citations

1. **Encyclopaedia Iranica**: *Ōrakzī*.
   - **Publication**: Columbia University.
   - **Details**: Scholarly entry detailing the Orakzai tribe, their geographic heartland in Tirah, and their classification within the Karlani branch of Pashtuns.
   - **Claim Supported**: Karlani classification, geographic association with Tirah, and traditional genealogical frameworks.

2. **Grierson, George A.**: *On the Tirahi Language*.
   - **Publication**: *Journal of the Royal Asiatic Society of Great Britain and Ireland*, Cambridge University Press.
   - **Details**: Historical linguistic study recording accounts of Tirahi speakers and their association with the Tirah country, including mentions of conflicts involving Orakzais and Afridis.
   - **Claim Supported**: Historical connection between Orakzai, Tirah, and neighboring communities; regional movement and conflict accounts.

3. **Khan, Muhammad Tahir & Khatoon, Dr. Bushra**: *A Comparative Lexical Study of Pashto Dialects (Dawarwala, Orakzai, Bangash)*.
   - **Publication**: *Palatana* (Malakand University Research Journal of Pashto), Vol. 4, No. 1, 2024.
   - **Details**: Comparative lexical analysis of Pashto varieties, discussing their traditional Karlani relationship and linguistic characteristics.
   - **Claim Supported**: Orakzai Pashto varieties within the Karlani genealogical and linguistic framework.

4. **1911 Encyclopædia Britannica**: *Orakzai*.
   - **Publication**: Cambridge University Press.
   - **Details**: Historical overview noting the traditional meaning of the name ("lost tribes") and the obscurity of their ancient origins.
   - **Claim Supported**: Etymological interpretations ("lost tribes") and the lack of a single universally accepted ancient origin story.

5. **Frontier and Overseas Expeditions from India**: *Volume II: North-West Frontier Tribes*.
   - **Publication**: Intelligence Branch, Army Headquarters India, 1908.
   - **Details**: Administrative and military record providing detailed descriptions of Orakzai tribal sections and historical geography.
   - **Claim Supported**: Tribal organization (seven main sections), settlement patterns in the Mastura and Khanki valleys.
