# Research Notes: Page 66 — The Role of Elders

## Core Concept
- **Elders as Social Anchors**: In Orakzai society, elders (*Masharan*) serve as the bridge between ancestral traditions and modern community life.
- **Multifaceted Roles**: Their influence spans family guidance, communal mediation (*Jirga*), hospitality leadership (*Hujra*), and the preservation of oral history.
- **Contextual Authority**: Authority is not absolute but earned through experience, wisdom, and the ability to maintain social cohesion.

## Key Findings

### 1. Masharan (Elders) and Social Cohesion
- **Social Function**: Elders are primary actors in promoting social cohesion within the Orakzai district. They act as "local supporters" who stabilize community relations during times of change or crisis.
- **Source**: *The Role of Local Supporters in Promoting Social Cohesion in District Orakzai* (J. Alam, 2024).

### 2. The Malik Institution
- **Definition**: Maliks are traditional tribal leaders who represent their communities in administrative and social affairs.
- **Orakzai Context**: In villages like Mirbak, Maliks remain central to managing tribal affairs and interfacing with state structures.
- **Source**: *Role of Malik in Pukhtoon Tribal Areas* (Infinity Press, study on village Mirbak).

### 3. Jirga and Mediation
- **Mechanism**: The Jirga is an assembly of elders (*Masharan*) that resolves disputes through collective deliberation.
- **Process**: Focuses on reconciliation (*Nanawatai*) and maintaining communal balance rather than purely punitive justice.
- **Source**: *Pashtun Jirga and Prospects of Peace and Conflict Resolution* (JSTOR, 2020).

### 4. Women and Elderhood
- **Internal Influence**: While public leadership is often male-dominated, elder women hold significant authority within the household (*Kor*).
- **Conflict Prevention**: Elder women often serve as "early warning" systems, identifying community tensions before they escalate into open conflict.
- **Source**: *Early Warning by Women for Conflict Prevention in the Erstwhile FATA* (Noreen Naseer, 2026).

### 5. Intergenerational Transmission
- **Hujra Setting**: The traditional guesthouse serves as the primary classroom where youth (*Kishray*) learn from the experiences and stories of the elders.
- **Values**: Transmission of *Pashtunwali* (the code of life), including hospitality (*Melmastia*), honor (*Nang*), and bravery (*Tura*).

## Evidence Hierarchy
- **A. Orakzai-Specific**: Studies on village Mirbak; J. Alam's 2024 research on Orakzai social cohesion.
- **B. Pashtun-Wide**: The general *Mashar/Kishar* dynamic and the Jirga institution.
- **C. Historical**: British-era administrative records on the Malik system.
- **D. Contemporary**: Digital adaptation of elder guidance via mobile communication.

## Documented Terminology
- **Mashar / Masharan**: Elder / Elders.
- **Kishar / Kishray**: Younger / Youth.
- **Malik**: Traditional community representative.
- **Jirga**: Council of elders.
- **Hujra**: Communal guesthouse.
- **Nanawatai**: Seeking protection/reconciliation through elders.
