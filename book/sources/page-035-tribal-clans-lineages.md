# Sources: Page 35 — Tribal Clans & Lineages

This document records the academic and historical sources supporting **Page 35: Tribal Clans & Lineages** in *I’M ORAKZAI*.

## Primary References & Academic Citations

1. **King, Lucas White**: *Monograph on the Orakzai Country and Clans*.
   - **Publication**: Punjab Government Press, 1900.
   - **Details**: The definitive colonial-era study of Orakzai tribal structure, providing an exhaustive list of clans, khels, and lineages.
   - **Claim Supported**: Identification of major sections (Ismailzai, Daulatzai, Alizai, Muhammad Khel, Sultanzai, Ali Khel, Malla Khel, Mishti, Sheikhan, Utman Khel, Sturi Khel, Firoz Khel, Bizoti) and their internal divisions.

2. **Balland, Daniel**: *Encyclopaedia Iranica*.
   - **Entry**: "DAWLATZĪ".
   - **Publication**: Vol. VII, Fasc. 2, 1994, pp. 150–151.
   - **Details**: Academic entry confirming Dawlatzī as one of the seven Orakzai subtribes and citing King (1900) for historical population estimates.
   - **Claim Supported**: Status of Dawlatzai as a foundational Orakzai section and the importance of colonial-era monographs in documenting tribal genealogy.

3. **Naval Postgraduate School (NPS)**: *Orakzai Tribe* (Program for Culture and Conflict Studies).
   - **Publication**: NPS Backgrounder / Fact Sheet.
   - **Details**: Modern ethnographic backgrounder identifying the six main clans and four hamsaya clans of the Orakzai.
   - **Claim Supported**: Prominence of Ali Khel as the largest section and the identification of Mamozai as a major Upper Orakzai section.

4. **1911 Encyclopædia Britannica**: *Orakzai*.
   - **Publication**: Cambridge University Press.
   - **Details**: Historical entry describing the Orakzai as consisting of refugee clans and noting their geographic distribution.
   - **Claim Supported**: Association of Orakzai groups with the Tirah region and the concept of "associated" clans.

## Research Notes on Clan Classification

- **Ali Khel**: Consistently documented as the largest and most demographically significant section in Upper Orakzai.
- **Mamozai**: A major section in Upper Orakzai, identified as the author's ancestral clan, with a distinct settlement pattern in the highland valleys.
- **Khel**: The term is used fluidly across all sources to denote kinship groups ranging from sub-sections to specific lineages.
- **Ham-saya**: Historically used to describe associated or protected groups (Mishti, Sheikhan, Malla Khel, Ali Khel), now fully integrated into the Orakzai identity.
