# Page 101 Research Notes — From Tribal Society to Modern Society

## Scope and Methodology

Page 101 explores the social transition of Orakzai society from traditional tribal frameworks to modern institutional structures. The methodology emphasizes "change without erasure," documenting how traditional institutions (kinship, elders, jirga, hujra) coexist and adapt within a modern civic, educational, and technological environment.

Research focuses on:
1.  **Foundational Social Structure**: Using Akbar S. Ahmed’s ethnographic research on Orakzai.
2.  **Institutional Transformation**: The 2018 FATA merger (25th Amendment) as a legal-administrative turning point.
3.  **Contemporary Development Evidence**: Documenting specific skills training programmes (UNDP/Japan 2024, 2025) as evidence of emerging social pathways.
4.  **Adaptation Framework**: Analyzing how family, language, and values continue while adapting to urbanization and migration.

## Key Research Findings

### 1. Traditional Social Institutions
- **Akbar S. Ahmed (1981, 2011)**: Highlights the importance of lineage, kinship, and the role of the Political Agent in Orakzai. His work provides the baseline for "tribal society" as a complex, self-sufficient social organization.
- **Jirga and Hujra**: Customary institutions for deliberation and social interaction. Research (Shinwari, 2025) indicates these remain relevant but operate within a changing legal landscape.

### 2. The 2018 FATA Merger
- **Constitutional Change**: The 25th Amendment formally integrated FATA into Khyber Pakhtunkhwa.
- **Social Impact**: Transition from the Frontier Crimes Regulation (FCR) to formal constitutional governance, policing, and judiciary.
- **Administrative Evolution**: Shift from Orakzai Agency to Orakzai District, introducing provincial public services and elected representation.

### 3. Contemporary Skills and Vocational Evidence
- **2024 Vocational Programme**: UNDP/Japan-supported training for 700 youth (375 women) in Kurram and Orakzai. Skills included mobile repair, solar systems, and LED assembly. (ReliefWeb, Nov 2024).
- **2025 Digital Skills Programme**: UNDP/Japan-supported training for 600 youth (52% women) in digital freelancing and remote work. (UNDP Press Release, April 2025).
- **Classification**: These are "Contemporary Development Initiatives" demonstrating specific pathways, not universal district-wide employment statistics.

### 4. Continuity and Adaptation
- **Multilingualism**: Coexistence of Pashto, Urdu, and English in modern life.
- **Urbanization**: Families in cities like Karachi or Peshawar maintain cultural identity while adapting to urban social networks.
- **Digital Hujra**: The concept of digital communication (WhatsApp, social media) allowing for cultural transmission and family support across distances.

## Research Gaps and Limitations
- **Village-Level Longitudinal Data**: Limited data on how specific Orakzai villages have changed socially over the last decade.
- **Impact of Merger on Jirga**: Ongoing academic debate on the effectiveness of traditional mediation vs. formal courts post-merger.
- **Gendered Social Change**: While development programmes report high women's participation, broader social change for women in rural Orakzai remains under-documented.
