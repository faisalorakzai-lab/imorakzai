# Sources: Page 54 — Traditional Clothing

This document records the academic and historical sources supporting **Page 54: Traditional Clothing** in *I’M ORAKZAI*.

## Primary References & Academic Citations

1. **Ahmad, M. F.**: *Revival in Diaspora: A thesis presented in partial fulfilment of the requirements for the degree of Master of Design at Massey University, Wellington, New Zealand*. (2025).
   - **Details**: Contemporary design research examining traditional dress patterns, specifically mentioning Orakzai dresses.
   - **Claim Supported**: Orakzai-specific dress patterns and motifs.

2. **Gilmour, B.**: *Warrior Poets: Guns, Movie-making and the Wild West of Pakistan*. (2013).
   - **Details**: Ethnographic account mentioning Orakzai Agency, waxed cloth designs, and hand-embroidery.
   - **Claim Supported**: Orakzai-specific embroidery and textile crafts.

3. **Shinwari, N. A.**: *Political and social change in Pakistan's tribal areas: Understanding Pashtun youth*. (2025).
   - **Details**: Analysis of youth identity in former FATA, including the blending of traditional and modern attire.
   - **Claim Supported**: Generational changes and modern adaptations of clothing.

4. **Encyclopaedia Iranica**: *Clothing (Pashtun)*.
   - **Details**: Authoritative entry on Pashtun garments, including khet partug and firaq partug.
   - **Claim Supported**: General Pashtun-wide clothing traditions.

5. **Historical Gazetteer of the Northwest Frontier**: *Tirah and Orakzai Regions*.
   - **Details**: British administrative records describing the appearance and dress of the tribes in Tirah.
   - **Claim Supported**: Historical dress descriptions (khet partug, turbans).
