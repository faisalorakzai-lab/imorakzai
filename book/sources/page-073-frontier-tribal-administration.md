# Sources: Page 73 — Frontier Tribal Administration

This document records the historical and academic sources supporting **Page 73: Frontier Tribal Administration** in *I’M ORAKZAI*.

## Primary References & Academic Citations

1. **Hopkins, B. D.** (2015). "The Frontier Crimes Regulation and Frontier Governmentality". *Journal of Asian Studies*.
   - **Details**: Academic analysis of the FCR as a colonial administrative tool for managing tribal territories.
   - **Claim Supported**: Origins and mechanisms of the FCR, including collective responsibility and the concentration of power in the Political Agent.

2. **Tripodi, Christian.** (2013). *Edge of Empire: The British Political Officer and Tribal Administration on the North-West Frontier 1877–1947*. Ashgate Publishing.
   - **Details**: Historical study of the Political Agent system and the dynamics of indirect rule.
   - **Claim Supported**: Responsibilities of Political Agents and the use of tribal intermediaries.

3. **Khan, S.** (2016). "Special Status of Tribal Areas (FATA): An Artificial Imperial Construct Bleeding Asia". *Slavic-Eurasian Research Center*.
   - **Details**: Historical overview of the administrative setup of tribal agencies, specifically mentioning Orakzai's status before 1973.
   - **Claim Supported**: Orakzai was part of FR Kohat before becoming an agency in 1973.

4. **Infinity Press.** (2023). "Role of Malik in Pukhtoon tribal areas: Research in Village Mirbak, Orakzai". *Journal of Social Sciences and Sustainable Development*.
   - **Details**: Ethnographic research on the Malik system and its interaction with the Political Agent in Orakzai.
   - **Claim Supported**: The functioning of the Malik system, allowances (Lungis), and the role of the Political Agent in recognition.

5. **Ahmad, I.** (2024). "Colonial policing and police administration in erstwhile Northwest Frontier Province and tribal areas of British India". *Cogent Social Sciences*.
   - **Details**: Study of the legal and security frameworks in the frontier, comparing settled and tribal areas.
   - **Claim Supported**: Distinction between ordinary provincial law and frontier regulations.

## Documented Terminology

| Term | Context | Historical Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Political Agent (PA)** | Administration | The chief colonial official holding executive and judicial power in a tribal agency. |
| **Malik** | Tribal Leadership | A recognized tribal intermediary acting as a bridge to the colonial state. |
| **Lungi** | Patronage | A financial allowance or honorary cloth given to a recognized Malik. |
| **FCR** | Law | Frontier Crimes Regulation; the special legal framework for tribal areas. |
| **Khassadar** | Security | A locally recruited tribal levy for security and protection of routes. |
