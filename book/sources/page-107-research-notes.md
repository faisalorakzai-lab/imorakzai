# Research Notes: Page 107 — Oral History & Technology

This document records the research findings and methodological decisions for **Page 107: Oral History & Technology**.

## 1. Oral History Methodology
- **Definition:** A technique for generating and preserving original, historically interesting information—primary source material—from personal recollections (Smithsonian Institution Archives).
- **Process:** Research → Contact → Consent → Pre-interview → Record → Transcribe → Translate → Review → Metadata → Preserve → Access.
- **Evidentiary Value:** Oral history is evidence of memory and experience, not necessarily verified historical fact. It requires corroboration with other sources (archival documents, photographs, maps).

## 2. Pashtun Oral Traditions
- **Significance:** Pashto culture is predominantly oral, with folk stories, poetry, and customary codes (Pashtunwali) passed down through generations (Citizens Archive of Pakistan).
- **Hujra Life:** Traditionally the center for storytelling and oral transmission of history and values.
- **Dialect Variation:** Pashto oral histories preserve linguistic nuances and regional vocabulary that formal texts may erase.

## 3. Digital Memory & Preservation
- **Infrastructure:** Requires uncompressed master files (WAV/FLAC), metadata (Dublin Core), and the 3-2-1 backup strategy (3 copies, 2 media, 1 offsite).
- **Accessibility:** Digital archives make voice recordings searchable through transcripts, timestamps, and keyword indexing.
- **AI Integration:** AI assists in speech-to-text (transcription) and translation but requires human review for low-resource languages like Pashto to avoid errors in tribal/place names and cultural idioms.

## 4. Ethical & Legal Frameworks
- **Informed Consent:** Must be ongoing and granular (permission to record, transcribe, publish, etc.).
- **Privacy:** Heightened care for living individuals, sensitive family disputes, and children's records.
- **Ownership:** Distinguishing between individual, family, and community control of archival material.

## 5. Evidence Boundaries & Cautions
- **Testimony vs. Fact:** Oral testimony is personal experience; it is not independently verified fact unless corroborated.
- **Synthetic Media:** Deepfakes and synthetic voices pose risks to historical authenticity. Real recordings must be preserved and labeled.
- **No Existing Archive:** The "Orakzai Oral History Archive" is a proposed conceptual model, not an existing institution.
