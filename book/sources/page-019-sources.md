# Sources: Page 19 — Nanawatai — Protection & Forgiveness

This document records the academic and historical sources supporting **Page 19: Nanawatai — Protection & Forgiveness** in *I’M ORAKZAI*.

## Primary References & Academic Citations

1. **Mikhail Pelevin (Cambridge University Press)**: *Seeking Protection and Reconciliation: A Pashtun Legal Custom in Recorded Tribal Histories*.
   - **Author**: Mikhail Pelevin (St. Petersburg State University).
   - **Publication**: *International Journal of Middle East Studies*, Vol. 54, Issue 4, 2022, pp. 687–704.
   - **Publisher**: Cambridge University Press.
   - **DOI**: 10.1017/S0020743823000016
   - **Details**: Examines *nənawāte* historically using early modern Pashto sources (*Tarikh-i Murassaʿ*, *Adam Khan aw Durkhaney*), framing it as a complex legal custom involving protection, mediation, and reconciliation.
   - **Claim Supported**: Historical evidence of Nanawatai in early modern Pashto texts and its role in conflict resolution.

2. **Encyclopaedia Iranica**: *JERGA and Customary Asylum*.
   - **Details**: Scholarly overview of Pashtun customary assemblies (*jerga*) and the role of *nanawātay* in requesting peace, asylum, forgiveness, or protection.
   - **Claim Supported**: Distinction between the jirga assembly and Nanawatai as an appeal for refuge.

3. **Anthropological & Historical Studies**: *Customary Mediation and Reconciliation Pathways*.
   - **Details**: Analysis of how customary norms balance reciprocity (*badal*) with conflict-reduction mechanisms (*nanawatai*) across Pashtun communities.
   - **Claim Supported**: The social logic of stopping feuds through customary appeal.
