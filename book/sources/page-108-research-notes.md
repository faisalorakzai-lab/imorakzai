# Research Notes: Page 108 — Building a Digital Orakzai Archive

This document records the research findings and methodological decisions for **Page 108: Building a Digital Orakzai Archive**.

## 1. Digital Archive Definition and Standards
- **Definition:** A digital archive is an organized system for preserving, describing, managing, and providing appropriate access to digital or digitized records (DPC Handbook).
- **Standards:**
    - **Metadata:** Dublin Core (ISO 15836) for descriptive metadata; OAI-PMH for harvesting.
    - **Image Interoperability:** IIIF (International Image Interoperability Framework) for high-resolution viewing and comparison.
    - **Preservation:** UNESCO/PERSIST guidelines for selection and long-term sustainability.
- **Resilience:** Implementation of the 3-2-1 backup strategy (3 copies, 2 media, 1 offsite) and regular fixity/checksum checks.

## 2. Community Archiving and Ethics
- **Governance:** Proposed Stewardship Board model involving community representatives, archivists, historians, and technologists.
- **Ethics:** Mukurtu-inspired models for community-controlled access, prioritizing informed consent and cultural sovereignty.
- **Sensitivity:** Distinction between open, limited, and restricted access based on privacy (children, identity documents) and cultural sensitivity.

## 3. Archival Workflows
- **Provenance:** Documenting the chain of custody (Source → Custody → Digitization → Archive).
- **Authenticity:** Ensuring digital integrity through stable identifiers (ARK/DOI) and metadata transparency.
- **AI Role:** AI as an assistant for OCR, transcription, and translation, but never as a final authority or a source of fabricated records.

## 4. Orakzai Context
- **Categories:** History, Oral History, Photographs, Documents, Maps, Language, Music, Poetry, Proverbs, Food, Clothing, Hospitality, Family Records, etc.
- **Place Names:** Documenting original Pashto/Urdu forms alongside historical and modern administrative references.
- **Evidence Boundaries:** The "Orakzai Digital Archive" is a **PROPOSED ARCHIVAL MODEL**, not an existing institution.

## 5. Evidence Boundaries & Cautions
- **Proposed vs. Existing:** Clearly label all architectures and workflows as proposed or illustrative.
- **No Fabrication:** Never use AI to generate missing historical records or genealogies.
- **Evidence Levels:** Proposed classification from Level A (Primary Document) to Level E (Unverified Claim).
