# Research Notes: Page 113 — Pakistan’s Digital Economy

This document records the research findings and methodological decisions for **Page 113: Pakistan’s Digital Economy**.

## 1. Digital Economy Framework
- **Definition:** Economic activity enabled or transformed by digital technologies, encompassing connectivity, software, digital services, e-commerce, fintech, data platforms, and AI.
- **Scope:** Extends beyond the IT industry to include digitally enabled sectors like agriculture, banking, education, and government.
- **Drivers:** Infrastructure (fiber, mobile networks), human capital (skills, education), and institutional trust (regulation, payments).

## 2. Key Economic Statistics (Reporting Period: 2024–2026)
- **IT Services Exports (2025–26 Economic Survey):**
    - Value: **US$3.4 billion**
    - Growth: **19.8%**
    - Share of Services Exports: **46.1%**
- **Telecom & Broadband (PTA/Economic Survey 2024–25):**
    - Broadband Subscribers: **147.2 million** (March 2025)
    - Broadband Penetration: **59.8%** (March 2025)
    - Cellular Population Coverage: **91%** (December 2024)
    - 3G/4G Population Signal Coverage: **>81%** (December 2024)
- **Telecom Sites (2024–25 Economic Survey):** **57,063** operational cell sites by December 2024, with **96.2%** supporting 4G.

## 3. Core Sectors and Emerging Trends
- **Fintech:** Driven by **Raast** (instant payment system) and mobile wallets (Easypaisa, JazzCash). Digital payments are transforming financial inclusion.
- **Freelancing:** Pakistan is a significant global player in digital services (software, design, marketing). Supported by government initiatives like **e-Rozgaar**.
- **Startups:** Emergence of ecosystems in fintech, e-commerce, and logistics, supported by the **Pakistan Startup Fund**.
- **AI & Data:** Identified as national priorities in the **National AI Policy (2025)** and **Digital Nation Pakistan** vision.

## 4. Orakzai and Digital Participation
- **Context:** Participation in the national digital economy through remote work, freelancing, and digital education.
- **Constraints:** The digital divide remains significant, influenced by geography, electricity reliability, and device affordability.
- **Potential:** Connectivity serves as a bridge for the diaspora and local youth to participate in global markets.

## 5. Evidence Boundaries
- **Reporting Periods:** Strict distinction between FY 2024-25 and FY 2025-26 data.
- **No Promotion:** Avoids presenting Pakistan as a "fully digital" economy; highlights real-world constraints and infrastructure gaps.
- **Verification:** Every statistic is cross-referenced with primary official sources (Ministry of Finance, SBP, PTA).
