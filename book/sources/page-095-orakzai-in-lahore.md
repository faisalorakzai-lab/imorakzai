# Sources: Page 95 — Orakzai in Lahore

This document records the primary and academic sources supporting **Page 95: Orakzai in Lahore** in *I’M ORAKZAI*.

## Primary Academic & Research Sources

1. **Khalid, A., & Khan, A. A.** (2026). *Family language policy and identity formation among migrant Pashto community in the multilingual context of Lahore*. *Humanities and Social Sciences Communications* (Nature).
   - **Details**: Investigates language ecology, ideology, and planning among migrant Pashto speakers in Lahore.
   - **Claim Supported**: Pashto as the desired home language; Urdu/English for social mobility; multilingualism as an identity resource.

2. **Ghafoor, A., et al.** (2023). *Urban Morphology and Green Spaces Changes in Model Town, The Oldest Settlement of Lahore, Using Satellite Data*. *Bulletin of Business and Economics*.
   - **Details**: Documents built-up area increase in Lahore from 455.45 km² to 807.40 km² (1990–2021).
   - **Claim Supported**: Lahore's urban expansion and built-up land growth figures.

3. **Ghafoor, N., & Akbar, M. R.** (2022). *Socioeconomic determinants of migration in the city of Lahore, Pakistan*. *Journal of the Knowledge Economy*.
   - **Details**: Identifies economic conditions, employment, and education as primary drivers of migration to Lahore.
   - **Claim Supported**: Socioeconomic motivations for internal migration to Lahore.

4. **University & Alumni Records** (2024–2026). *Student and Alumni Engagement Records (PU, LUMS, GCU, UMT)*.
   - **Details**: Verified presence of Orakzai students and professionals (e.g., Fahad Orakzai, Huzaifa Waqar Orakzai).
   - **Claim Supported**: Specific Orakzai professional and educational presence in Lahore.

## Research Gaps & Limitations
- **Orakzai-Specific Population**: While Pashtun migration is well-documented, the specific Orakzai population count in Lahore is not isolated in public census data.
- **Neighborhood Mapping**: Orakzai presence is dispersed across the city; no "Orakzai-majority" sectors are documented in Lahore.
- **Linguistic Nuance**: Research on family language policy covers the "migrant Pashto community" generally; Orakzai-specific dialectal variations in Lahore are not yet documented.
