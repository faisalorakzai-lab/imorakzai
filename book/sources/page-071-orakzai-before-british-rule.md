# Sources: Page 71 — Orakzai Before British Rule

This document records the academic and historical sources supporting **Page 71: Orakzai Before British Rule** in *I’M ORAKZAI*.

## Primary References & Academic Citations

1. **King, Lucas White.** (1900). *Monograph on the Orakzai Country and Clans*. Lahore: Printed at the Punjab Government Press.
   - **Details**: The definitive colonial-era study of Orakzai tribal structure, clans, and territory.
   - **Claim Supported**: Tribal organization, clan breakdown (18 clans), and geographic descriptions of Tirah.

2. **Stewart, Jules.** (2007). *The Savage Border: The Story of the North-West Frontier*. Sutton Publishing.
   - **Details**: Historical account of the frontier tribes, including Orakzai resistance and the Roshaniyya movement.
   - **Claim Supported**: Orakzai involvement in the Roshaniyya movement and resistance to Mughal rule.

3. **Qadir, Altaf & Minhas, Z.** (2013). "Khyber Pass in Imperial Politics of the Mughals (1519-1707)". *Journal of the Research Society of Pakistan*.
   - **Details**: Academic study of Mughal frontier policy and tribal resistance.
   - **Claim Supported**: Earliest documentary mentions of Orakzai in Mughal chronicles like *Akbarnama*.

4. **Bellew, H. W.** (1880). *The Races of Afghanistan: Being a Brief Account of the Principal Nations Inhabiting that Country*. Calcutta: Thacker, Spink & Co.
   - **Details**: Ethnographic and historical overview of Pashtun tribes under Durrani rule.
   - **Claim Supported**: Orakzai relationship with the Durrani Empire and negotiated autonomy.

5. **Rieck, Andreas.** (2015). *The Shias of Pakistan: An Assertive and Beleaguered Minority*. Oxford University Press.
   - **Details**: Documents the religious history of the region, including the conversion of Orakzai sub-sections.
   - **Claim Supported**: Influence of Tirah Sayyids and sectarian diversity in Orakzai.

## Documented Terminology

| Term | Context | Historical Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Tirah** | Geography | The ancestral and strategic heartland of the Orakzai. |
| **Roshaniyya** | Political | The 16th-century movement against Mughal centralism. |
| **Orakzai** | Etymology | "The Lost Son" (Oral tradition/legendary origin). |
| **Jirga** | Custom | The deliberative assembly for tribal governance. |
