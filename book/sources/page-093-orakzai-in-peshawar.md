# Sources: Page 93 — Orakzai in Peshawar

This document records the primary and academic sources supporting **Page 93: Orakzai in Peshawar** in *I’M ORAKZAI*.

## Primary Academic & Research Sources

1. **Mosel, I., & Jackson, A.** (2013). *Sanctuary in the city? Urban displacement and vulnerability in Peshawar, Pakistan*. Overseas Development Institute (ODI).
   - **Details**: Analyzes urban displacement and the vulnerability of tribal migrants in Peshawar.
   - **Claim Supported**: Peshawar as a destination for tribal IDPs and the challenges of urban adaptation.

2. **Naval Postgraduate School (NPS).** (Unknown). *Orakzai Tribal Profile*.
   - **Details**: Notes Orakzai holdings in Peshawar district and their regional presence.
   - **Claim Supported**: Geographical presence and historical settlement in the Peshawar region.

3. **Ul Hassan, S. M.** (2015). *Pakistani migration to the Gulf and development in the Miranzai valley in Pakistan*. In *Migration and Development in South Asia*.
   - **Details**: Documents the role of the University of Peshawar as a research site and educational destination for Orakzai students.
   - **Claim Supported**: Peshawar's role as an educational center for the Orakzai community.

4. **Bukhari, A. Q.** (2026). *Layered Continuity in Pakistan's Northwest Borderlands: State, Power, and Political Economy*.
   - **Details**: Discusses the persistence of tribal identity in the urban context of Peshawar.
   - **Claim Supported**: The concept of "layered continuity" and urban tribal networks.

## Research Gaps & Limitations
- **Statistical Breakdown**: There is no official census data that provides a precise count of Orakzai residents within the Peshawar metropolitan area.
- **Economic Quantities**: While business ownership is documented (e.g., real estate, construction), the total economic volume of Orakzai-specific trade in Peshawar is not statistically isolated.
- **Neighborhood Dominance**: While Orakzai families live in specific areas (e.g., Hayatabad, Pathan Colony), they share these spaces with other Pashtun tribes; no single urban neighborhood is exclusively Orakzai.
