# Sources: Page 82 — 1947 & the New Pakistan

This document records the administrative, academic, and historical sources supporting **Page 82: 1947 & the New Pakistan** in *I’M ORAKZAI*.

## Administrative & Constitutional Records

1. **Government of Pakistan.** (1949). *The Objectives Resolution*.
   - **Details**: Foundational document defining the Islamic and democratic principles of the Pakistani state.
   - **Claim Supported**: Ideological framework for national identity.

2. **Government of Pakistan.** (1955). *The Establishment of West Pakistan Act (One Unit Scheme)*.
   - **Details**: Reorganization of provinces into a single unit, while maintaining the special status of tribal areas.
   - **Claim Supported**: 1955 administrative reorganization.

3. **District Courts Orakzai.** (2024). "About the District." *Official Website*.
   - **Details**: Historical record stating Orakzai was part of Frontier Region (FR) Kohat until 1973.
   - **Claim Supported**: Orakzai's administrative status as part of FR Kohat in 1947.

## Academic & Historical Studies

4. **Haroon, S.** (2021). *The Pashtun Borderlands: Development, Nation, and Agency 1947–55*. Edinburgh University Press.
   - **Details**: Analysis of early Pakistan's frontier policy and the withdrawal of troops (Operation Curzon).
   - **Claim Supported**: Jinnah's tribal policy and institutional continuity.

5. **Haroon, S.** (2022). "Tribal Elders and Societal Inequities in Pakistan's Northwestern Borderlands (1947-2018)." *Archives de sciences sociales des religions*.
   - **Details**: Examination of how tribal society was administered as a frontier by both colonial and Pakistani states.
   - **Claim Supported**: Maintenance of the Political Agency system and FCR.

6. **Cheema, M., & Yousaf, F.** (2020). "Constitutionalizing a perpetual transition: The 'integration' of the Pashtun 'tribal areas' in Pakistan." *International Journal of Constitutional Law*.
   - **Details**: Legal analysis of the semi-autonomous status of tribal areas after 1947.
   - **Claim Supported**: Post-independence administrative continuity.
