# Research Notes: Page 76 — Frontier Politics

## Core Concept
- **Negotiated Authority**: Frontier politics is a system of continuous negotiation between local tribal authority and state power.
- **Intermediary System**: The role of Maliks and Jirgas as bridges between the community and the Political Agent (PA).
- **Institutional Change**: The evolution from pre-colonial distributed authority to the formal Orakzai Agency (est. 1973) and the eventual FATA merger (2018).

## Key Findings

### 1. The Creation of Orakzai Agency (1973)
- **Establishment**: Orakzai Agency was formally inaugurated on December 1, 1973, following an announcement by PM Zulfiqar Ali Bhutto at a grand tribal jirga.
- **Uniqueness**: It was the only agency in the former FATA created after the independence of Pakistan, having previously been managed through the frontier districts of Kohat and Peshawar.
- **Source**: *Orakzai District* (Wikipedia/Official KP Records); *Overview of Orakzai Agency* (Scribd).

### 2. The Malik System
- **Role**: Maliks served as government-recognized intermediaries. They distributed frontier allowances (*muajib*) and were responsible for collecting fines (*chatti*) imposed on the tribe.
- **Authority**: While appointed by the state, their legitimacy often depended on their lineage and ability to influence their respective clans.
- **Source**: *Role of Malik in Pukhtoon Tribal Areas* (ResearchGate, 2025; Infinity Press).

### 3. The Jirga as a Political Forum
- **Function**: A collective council of elders for deliberation, dispute resolution, and representing the tribe to the Political Agent.
- **State Interaction**: The British and later Pakistani administrations used the Jirga to secure promises, manage security, and implement administrative decisions.
- **Source**: *Jirga System and Its Role in Peacebuilding* (J. Alam, 2021).

### 4. The Political Agent (PA)
- **Power**: Concentrated executive, judicial, and political authority. The PA managed tribal relations through the "carrot and stick" approach (allowances vs. punitive actions).
- **Ethnographic Insight**: Dr. Akbar S. Ahmed served as the PA in Orakzai (1976-77) and documented the agency's social and political structure in his academic work.
- **Source**: *The Arab Connection: Emergent Models of Social Structure* (Akbar S. Ahmed, 1981).

### 5. Kinship and Sectional Interests
- **Internal Diversity**: The Orakzai tribe is not a monolith. Different sections (e.g., Bizoti, Alikhel, Mamozai) often had divergent political priorities and alliances.
- **Lineage Politics**: Political influence was frequently tied to the strength and unity of a specific lineage or clan.

## Evidence Hierarchy
- **A. Administrative Records**: Gazetteers, PA reports, and official government announcements (1973 inauguration).
- **B. Academic Scholarship**: Akbar S. Ahmed (1981) on Orakzai social structure; M. Hayat (2009) on tribal maliks.
- **C. Legal Framework**: The Frontier Crimes Regulation (FCR) 1901 and the 25th Amendment (2018).

## Source Criticism
- **Institutional Bias**: Administrative reports often focus on "control" and "compliance," potentially downplaying internal tribal grievances.
- **Elite Focus**: Records tend to highlight the actions of Maliks and recognized elders, often overlooking the political sentiments of the wider community.
