# Research Notes: Page 89 — Migration & Urbanization

## Core Concept
- **Migration as a Multi-Dimensional Force**: Migration in Orakzai is not a singular experience but a complex response to education, employment, trade, health, and security needs.
- **Urbanization as Transformation**: Beyond physical movement to cities, urbanization in Orakzai involves the administrative and economic transformation of local centers like Kalaya and Ghiljo.
- **The Diaspora Connection**: The Orakzai identity is increasingly trans-local, maintained through digital networks and social ties between the highland villages and urban hubs like Karachi, Peshawar, and the Gulf.

## Key Migration Patterns

### 1. Internal Migration (Regional)
- **Primary Hubs**: Kohat, Hangu (Miranzai Valley), and Peshawar are the first-tier destinations due to proximity and historical ties.
- **Property Acquisition**: Documented evidence of Orakzai tribesmen purchasing property and establishing businesses in Hangu and Kohat, creating a "peri-homeland" diaspora.
- **Educational Migration**: Students moving to Peshawar and Islamabad for higher education, often leading to permanent professional settlement.

### 2. Long-Distance Internal Migration (National)
- **Karachi**: The largest Orakzai diaspora hub outside the northwest. Major concentrations in Sohrab Goth, Banaras, and Pathan Colony.
- **Occupations**: Heavy involvement in the transport sector (trucking, mini-buses), retail, and construction labor.
- **Sectarian Displacement**: Historical conflict and sectarian dynamics (e.g., 2008-2012) forced significant numbers of Orakzai families to seek safety in Karachi and Peshawar.

### 3. International Migration (Global)
- **Gulf Countries (GCC)**: Significant labor migration to the UAE and Saudi Arabia.
- **Remittances**: A primary driver of household resilience, funding food security, private education, healthcare, and the construction of modern "remittance houses" in the valleys.

## Demographic Data (2023 Census Context)
- **Total Population**: 387,561 (2023 Digital Census).
- **Household Count**: 52,104.
- **Rural/Urban Split**: Classified as **100% Rural** in the 2023 Census locality tables (78 rural localities). However, administrative centers like **Kalaya** and **Ghiljo** function as emerging urban nodes.
- **Population Density**: ~252 persons per km², reflecting high concentration in fertile valleys.

## Urbanization Components
- **Administrative Reclassification**: The transition from Agency to District (2018) has accelerated the urbanization of Kalaya as a seat of government services.
- **Economic Transformation**: Growth of markets (e.g., Ghiljo, Mishti Mela) into permanent commercial hubs.
- **Digital Urbanism**: The use of mobile networks to bridge the gap between rural residence and urban connectivity.

## Author's Reflection Themes
- **The Trans-local Identity**: Being Orakzai is no longer defined solely by residence in the mountains; it is carried into the streets of Karachi and the labor camps of Dubai.
- **Memory and Return**: The dream of "returning" to the village often fuels the hard work in the city, even if the return is only for holidays or retirement.
- **Resilience through Movement**: Migration is not a sign of failure but a strategy for survival and advancement in a challenging geographic landscape.
