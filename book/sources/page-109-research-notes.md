# Research Notes: Page 109 — Mapping Orakzai History

This document records the research findings and methodological decisions for **Page 109: Mapping Orakzai History**.

## 1. Geographic and Administrative Context
- **Coordinates:** 33° 33′ to 33° 54′ N, 70° 33′ to 71° 15′ E.
- **Area:** 1,538 square kilometers.
- **Administrative Evolution:** 
    - Pre-1973: Part of the Frontier Region (FR) Kohat.
    - 1973–2018: Orakzai Agency (FATA).
    - 2018–Present: Orakzai District, Kohat Division, Khyber Pakhtunkhwa (KP).
- **Boundaries:** Khyber District (North), Kohat District (South), Peshawar District (East), Kurram District (West).

## 2. Historical Mapping Sources
- **Survey of India (SOI):** Topographical maps (1/4 inch, 1 inch scales) from the British India period (e.g., IOR/X/14736/1).
- **Trans-Frontier Maps:** 51 historical maps (1880–1910) showing the "Frontier Areas" (Zenodo/SOI).
- **Gazetteers:** Imperial Gazetteer of India, NWFP Gazetteers (Kohat District sections).
- **Modern Resources:** OCHA Pakistan administrative boundary maps (2023), Survey of Pakistan, Pakistan Bureau of Statistics (PBS).

## 3. Topography and Physical Geography
- **Terrain:** Predominantly hilly/mountainous terrain.
- **Elevation:** Ranging from approximately 2,000 to 3,000 meters.
- **Key Features:** Mountain passes, river valleys (e.g., Mastura Valley), and watersheds that historically influenced tribal boundaries and migration routes.

## 4. Methodology: Historical GIS (HGIS)
- **Place Name Authority:** Addressing multiple spellings (Pashto, Urdu, English) and transliteration variants.
- **Georeferencing:** Aligning historical SOI maps with modern coordinate systems (WGS84) using control points like mountain peaks and river junctions.
- **Layering:** Conceptualizing history as layers of evidence (Topography → Settlements → Administrative Boundaries → Migration → Memory).

## 5. Evidence Boundaries & Cautions
- **Schematic Maps:** All maps in this book are **SCHEMATIC** and **ILLUSTRATIVE**. They are not definitive historical boundary maps.
- **Disputed Boundaries:** Avoid redrawing disputed or uncertain tribal/administrative boundaries as definitive.
- **No Fabrication:** Do not invent historical settlements or migration routes without corroborating evidence from gazetteers or oral histories.
- **Place Names:** Acknowledge that a place remembered by a family may not appear on official administrative maps.
