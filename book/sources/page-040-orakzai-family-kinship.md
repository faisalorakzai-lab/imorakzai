# Sources: Page 40 — Orakzai Family & Kinship

This document records the academic and historical sources supporting **Page 40: Orakzai Family & Kinship** in *I’M ORAKZAI*.

## Primary References & Academic Citations

1. **King, Lucas White**: *Monograph on the Orakzai Country and Clans*.
   - **Publication**: Punjab Government Press, 1900.
   - **Details**: Provides foundational observations on the social organization, clan (khel) structures, and kinship patterns of the Orakzai sections.
   - **Claim Supported**: Historical Orakzai kinship networks and tribal divisions.

2. **Khan, Sarfraz**: *Proposed Gender Inclusive Reforms in FATA*.
   - **Publication**: *SSRN / Central Asia*, 2016.
   - **Details**: Discusses the interaction between *Rawaj* (customary law) and *Sharia* (Islamic law) in the tribal areas, specifically regarding women's inheritance and social status.
   - **Claim Supported**: Tension between customary land inheritance (male-only) and Islamic law entitlements; structural terminology (quom, khel, tapa).

3. **Asghar, W.**: *FATA Merger: Impacts on Status of Tribal Women*.
   - **Publication**: *Journal of Development and Social Sciences (JDSS)*, 2024.
   - **Details**: Examines the economic and social status of Orakzai women, emphasizing the role of inheritance and family dependence.
   - **Claim Supported**: Economic status of women in Orakzai; importance of inheritance determinants.

4. **UN Women / Asian Development Bank**: *Status of Women and Girls in Orakzai District*.
   - **Publication**: 2020.
   - **Details**: Modern report on household dynamics, gendered social spaces, and the role of women in family care and cultural reproduction in Orakzai.
   - **Claim Supported**: Modern Orakzai household composition; gender roles within the family.

5. **eHRAF World Cultures**: *Culture Summary: Pashtun*.
   - **URL**: https://ehrafworldcultures.yale.edu/cultures/au04/summary
   - **Details**: Detailed ethnographic overview of Pashtun kinship, family systems, and inheritance patterns (land division among males).
   - **Claim Supported**: General Pashtun patterns of unilineal descent and male-centered inheritance.

6. **Haroon, Sana**: *Frontier of Faith: Islam in the Indo-Afghan Borderland*.
   - **Publication**: Columbia University Press, 2011.
   - **Details**: Discusses the role of *Sharia* as a basis for social practice in the frontier regions and its interaction with customary law.
   - **Claim Supported**: Historical role of religious scholars in interpreting family law and social norms.

## Research Notes on Family & Kinship

- **Household vs. Family**: The household (*kor*) is the basic domestic unit, while family (*kuranay*) extends to a broader network of kin.
- **Inheritance Tension**: Customary practice often prioritizes keeping land within the male lineage (patrilineal), while Islamic law grants women specific shares. This remains a key area of social and legal negotiation.
- **Marriage Networks**: Marriage (*nikah*) is a family-wide event that creates new alliances and networks (*khwakhi-zuri*).
- **Women's Centrality**: Despite public-facing male dominance, women are the primary transmitters of language, stories, and cultural values within the domestic sphere.
- **Digital Kinship**: Modern Orakzai families use digital tools (WhatsApp, video calls) to maintain "remote kinship" across global diasporas, bridging the gap between ancestral territory and new homes.
