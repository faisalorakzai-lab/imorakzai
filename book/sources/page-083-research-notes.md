# Research Notes: Page 83 — Tribal Areas & Pakistan

## Core Concept
- **Historical Administrative Relationship**: Explaining the unique constitutional and administrative status of the Tribal Areas (FATA) from 1947 until the 2018 merger.
- **Orakzai's Place**: Orakzai was one of the seven federal agencies, created in 1973, marking a shift from a Frontier Region (FR) to a dedicated Agency.
- **The Merger (2018)**: The 25th Constitutional Amendment as the culmination of decades of reform debate, integrating FATA into Khyber Pakhtunkhwa (KP).

## Key Findings

### 1. Constitutional Framework (1973-2018)
- **Article 246**: Defined the "Tribal Areas," distinguishing between Federally Administered Tribal Areas (FATA) and Provincially Administered Tribal Areas (PATA).
- **Article 247**: Granted the President of Pakistan executive authority over FATA. No Act of Parliament or Provincial Assembly applied to FATA unless specifically directed by the President. This article was the legal basis for the special administrative status.
- **Repeal**: Article 247 was repealed by the **25th Constitutional Amendment** in May 2018.

### 2. The Seven Agencies of FATA
- The FATA region historically consisted of seven administrative agencies:
  1. **Bajaur**
  2. **Mohmand**
  3. **Khyber**
  4. **Orakzai** (Created 1973)
  5. **Kurram**
  6. **North Waziristan**
  7. **South Waziristan**
- Additionally, there were six Frontier Regions (FRs) attached to adjacent settled districts (Peshawar, Kohat, Bannu, Lakki Marwat, Tank, Dera Ismail Khan).

### 3. Orakzai Agency History
- **Creation**: Announced by Prime Minister Zulfikar Ali Bhutto on **November 3, 1973**, and formally inaugurated on **December 1, 1973**.
- **Pre-1973 Status**: Orakzai territory was part of **FR Kohat**.
- **Administrative Seat**: Historically managed from Hangu/Kohat due to its landlocked geography.

### 4. The Administrative System
- **Political Agent (PA)**: The executive head of the agency, exercising administrative, judicial, and development powers.
- **Malik System**: Government-recognized tribal intermediaries who received allowances (muajibs) and lungis in exchange for maintaining tribal responsibility and security.
- **Frontier Crimes Regulation (FCR)**: A colonial-era legal code (1901) that remained in force until 2018, characterized by "collective responsibility" and the absence of ordinary judicial oversight.

### 5. The 2018 Merger (25th Amendment)
- **Process**: Approved by the National Assembly, Senate, and the KP Provincial Assembly in May 2018.
- **Outcome**: FATA merged into KP; Orakzai Agency became **Orakzai District**.
- **Institutional Shift**: Extension of the jurisdiction of the Supreme Court and Peshawar High Court to the former tribal areas; transition from Political Agent to Deputy Commissioner (DC).

## Author's Reflection Themes
- **Integration as a Process**: Constitutional merger is a legal event; administrative and social integration is a multi-generational process.
- **From Agency to District**: The shift in terminology reflects a shift in the relationship between the citizen and the state.
- **Memory of FATA**: Preserving the history of the special status while moving toward a unified legal framework.
