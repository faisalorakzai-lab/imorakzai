# Sources: Page 23 — Badal — Justice & Revenge

This document records the academic and historical sources supporting **Page 23: Badal — Justice & Revenge** in *I’M ORAKZAI*.

## Primary References & Academic Citations

1. **Bruce L. Benson & Zafar R. Siddiqui**: *Pashtunwali—Law for the lawless, defense for the stateless*.
   - **Authors**: Bruce L. Benson and Zafar R. Siddiqui.
   - **Publication**: *International Review of Law and Economics*, Volume 37, March 2014, pp. 108–120.
   - **Publisher**: Elsevier.
   - **DOI**: 10.1016/j.irle.2013.07.008
   - **Details**: Examines Pashtunwali as a stateless legal system, analyzing badal as retaliation and decentralized enforcement, alongside customary mechanisms like thega, melmastia, and nanawatey.
   - **Claim Supported**: Badal as customary law, deterrence, and decentralized enforcement.

2. **Anders Widmark**: *Experiences of Revenge as Reflected in the Contemporary Pashto Short Story*.
   - **Author**: Anders Widmark.
   - **Publication**: *Best Served Cold: Studies on Revenge*, 2010, pp. 153–161.
   - **Publisher**: Brill / Rodopi.
   - **DOI**: 10.1163/9781848880436_018
   - **Details**: Analyzes the contemporary Pashto short story genre and warns against treating Pashtun revenge as a static or simplistic characteristic, exploring badal in relation to honour and cultural memory.
   - **Claim Supported**: Nuanced literary and cultural perspectives on revenge and honour in Pashtun society.

3. **Lutz Rzehak**: *Doing Pashto: Pashtunwali as the Ideal of Honourable Behaviour and Tribal Life among the Pashtuns*.
   - **Author**: Lutz Rzehak.
   - **Publication**: Afghanistan Analysts Network, 2011.
   - **Details**: Explores the ethical norms of Pashtunwali and the structural role of reciprocity and customary expectations.
   - **Claim Supported**: Customary expectations and normative behavior.
