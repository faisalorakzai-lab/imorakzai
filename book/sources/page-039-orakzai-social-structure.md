# Sources: Page 39 — Orakzai Social Structure

This document records the academic and historical sources supporting **Page 39: Orakzai Social Structure** in *I’M ORAKZAI*.

## Primary References & Academic Citations

1. **King, Lucas White**: *Monograph on the Orakzai Country and Clans*.
   - **Publication**: Punjab Government Press, 1900.
   - **Details**: Provides a detailed breakdown of Orakzai sections, clans (khels), and lineages, documenting the social organization of the tribe in the late 19th century.
   - **Claim Supported**: Tribal sections and khel divisions; historical social hierarchy and kinship patterns.

2. **Khan, Sarfraz**: *A Case for Gender Inclusive Reforms*.
   - **Publication**: *Central Asia*, Issue 65.
   - **Details**: Discusses the patriarchal social and administrative structures in the tribal areas, explaining the division of land into quom (tribe), khel (clan), tapa, and kandae (sub-clan).
   - **Claim Supported**: Structural terminology (quom, khel, tapa, kandae); patriarchal nature of social organization.

3. **UN Women / Asian Development Bank**: *Status of Women and Girls in Orakzai District*.
   - **Publication**: 2020.
   - **Details**: Provides modern insights into household structures, gender roles, and the social environment in Orakzai District.
   - **Claim Supported**: Household composition; gendered social spaces; modern social challenges.

4. **Pakistan Almanac**: *Khyber Pakhtunkhwa - Orakzai*.
   - **URL**: https://pakistanalmanac.com/kp-orakzai/
   - **Details**: Records modern demographic data, including average household size (8.8 persons) and economic livelihoods in the region.
   - **Claim Supported**: Household size; economic life and livelihoods.

5. **eHRAF World Cultures**: *Culture Summary: Pashtun*.
   - **URL**: https://ehrafworldcultures.yale.edu/cultures/au04/summary
   - **Details**: Comprehensive ethnographic summary of Pashtun social organization, defining terms like khel and the role of kinship.
   - **Claim Supported**: Definition of khel; role of kinship and residential unity in social groupings.

## Research Notes on Social Organization

- **Structural Hierarchy**: Person → Household → Family → Kinship → Khel (Clan) → Tapa (Section) → Quom (Tribe).
- **Khel (Clan)**: The primary unit of social and residential unity within a section.
- **Household**: Typically male-headed and extended, serving as the basic unit of economic and social life.
- **Kinship**: A melding of descent, marriage, and locality that shapes social obligations and support networks.
- **Women's Agency**: While formal institutions are male-dominated, women are central to family, household, and the transmission of cultural identity.
- **Social Change**: Urbanization and migration (diaspora) are transforming traditional structures while maintaining cultural continuity through digital networks.
