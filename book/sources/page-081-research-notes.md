# Research Notes: Page 81 — Orakzai During the Creation of Pakistan

## Core Concept
- **1947 Transition**: The constitutional and administrative shift from British paramountcy to the Dominion of Pakistan.
- **Tribal Status**: The unique legal position of the Tribal Areas (including the Orakzai region) under the Indian Independence Act 1947.
- **Institutional Continuity**: The preservation of the Political Agency system and the Frontier Crimes Regulation (FCR) in the post-independence era.

## Key Findings

### 1. The Constitutional Break (1947)
- **Indian Independence Act 1947, Section 7(1)(c)**: Explicitly stated that "there shall lapse also any treaties or agreements in force at the date of the passing of this Act between His Majesty and any persons having authority in the tribal areas."
- **Paramountcy**: British authority over the tribal areas was not "sovereignty" in the Westphalian sense but "paramountcy" based on negotiated treaties. When these lapsed, the tribal areas technically regained their pre-treaty status until new agreements were signed with Pakistan.
- **Source**: *Indian Independence Act 1947*; *The special status of the federally administered tribal areas of Pakistan* (ResearchGate).

### 2. The NWFP Referendum (July 1947)
- **Scope**: The referendum was conducted in the **Settled Districts** of the North-West Frontier Province (NWFP).
- **Tribal Participation**: Tribal areas were not part of the formal electorate for the referendum. However, the result (favoring Pakistan) set the regional political direction.
- **Orakzai Context**: As part of the Kohat Frontier Region (not yet a separate Agency), Orakzai communities were outside the settled district voting process but were influenced by the surrounding political mobilization.
- **Source**: *1947 North-West Frontier Province referendum* (Wikipedia); *Electoral Politics in the NWFP* (Sage, 2014).

### 3. Jinnah's Tribal Policy (1947-1948)
- **Agreements**: Muhammad Ali Jinnah, as Governor-General, signed new agreements with tribal jirgas in late 1947 and early 1948. These agreements generally maintained the existing administrative structure (Political Agents, Maliks) in exchange for tribal allegiance to Pakistan.
- **Withdrawal of Troops**: In a significant symbolic move, Jinnah ordered the withdrawal of regular army troops from the tribal areas (Operation Curzon), entrusting the security of the frontier to the tribal militias (Scouts) and the tribes themselves.
- **Source**: *Framework for peace pathways...* (SAS-Space); *Special status of Tribal Areas* (Khan, SSRN).

### 4. Orakzai's Specific Administrative Path
- **Kohat Frontier Region**: In 1947, Orakzai territory was administered as part of the "Frontier Region Kohat" (FR Kohat).
- **Agency Status**: Unlike Khyber or Waziristan, Orakzai did not have its own Political Agent in 1947. The **Orakzai Agency** was not formally carved out until **1973**.
- **Source**: *Orakzai District* (Wikipedia); *Special Status of Tribal Areas* (Khan, SSRN).

### 5. Regional Geopolitics
- **Afghanistan's Position**: Afghanistan was the only country to vote against Pakistan's admission to the UN in 1947, citing the Durand Line dispute and the status of the Pashtun tribes.
- **Pashtunistan**: The demand for an independent Pashtun state (or greater autonomy) influenced the political discourse, though the majority of tribal jirgas opted for Pakistan.
- **Source**: *Constitutionalizing a perpetual transition* (Cheema & Yousaf, 2020).

## Author's Reflection Themes
- **Transition vs. Moment**: 1947 was a constitutional moment but a social transition.
- **Institutional Memory**: The state changed, but the malik and jirga systems provided continuity.
- **Identity**: The overlapping identities of being Orakzai, Pashtun, Muslim, and Pakistani.
