# Research Notes: Page 73 — Frontier Tribal Administration

## Core Concept
- **Frontier Administration as a Negotiated System**: The British colonial state managed the Pashtun frontier through a distinctive framework that combined state officials (Political Agents), tribal intermediaries (Maliks), special laws (FCR), and customary institutions (Jirga).
- **Administrative Unit**: Historically, Orakzai was part of the **Frontier Region (FR) Kohat** until the formal creation of the Orakzai Agency in December 1973.
- **The Political Agent (PA)**: The central colonial official in a tribal agency, holding executive, judicial, and security powers. The PA was the bridge between the government and the tribes.

## Key Findings

### 1. The Frontier Crimes Regulation (FCR)
- **Origins**: Promulgated in 1872, 1887, and finalized in 1901 to provide a legal framework for governing tribal areas where ordinary British law was considered unsuitable.
- **Mechanisms**: Concentrated power in the Political Agent; used the **Jirga** as a judicial council; imposed **Collective Responsibility** (punishing a clan for an individual's actions).
- **Impact**: Created a "special" legal zone that lasted until 2018.
- **Source**: *The Frontier Crimes Regulation and Frontier Governmentality* (B.D. Hopkins, 2015).

### 2. The Malik System
- **Definition**: The British recognized specific tribal leaders (Maliks) as official intermediaries.
- **Patronage**: Maliks were paid **allowances (Lungis)** every six months to ensure cooperation and road safety.
- **Legitimacy**: Colonial recognition was essential for a Malik's official standing, but it did not always translate to local tribal legitimacy.
- **Source**: *Role of Malik in Pukhtoon tribal areas* (Infinity Press, Village Mirbak research).

### 3. Political Agents and Indirect Rule
- **Indirect Rule**: The state projected influence without full annexation, relying on local structures.
- **Responsibilities**: Negotiation, intelligence, security coordination, and dispute mediation.
- **Orakzai Example**: Muhammad Akbar Khan Orakzai served as an assistant Political Officer in the 1880s, illustrating the use of local notables in governance.
- **Source**: *Edge of Empire: The British Political Officer and Tribal Administration* (C. Tripodi, 2013).

### 4. Security: Levies and Scouts
- **Khassadars/Levies**: Locally recruited tribal forces managed by the Political Agent for maintaining order and protecting communication lines.
- **Scouts**: More formalized paramilitary units (e.g., Kurram Militia, Afridi Scouts) used for frontier defense.

## Evidence Hierarchy
- **A. Administrative Records**: FCR 1901 text, Punjab/NWFP gazetteers.
- **B. Institutional Studies**: Research on the Malik system in Orakzai (Village Mirbak).
- **C. Academic Scholarship**: Hopkins (2015) on governmentality; Tripodi (2013) on Political Officers.
- **D. Regional Context**: Studies of the Khyber and Kohat frontier administration.

## Documented Terminology
- **Agency**: The administrative territory governed by a Political Agent.
- **Frontier Region (FR)**: A buffer zone between a settled district and a tribal agency.
- **Collective Responsibility**: The legal principle of holding a group accountable for the acts of its members.
- **Lungi**: A term used for the allowance or honorary turban cloth given to recognized Maliks.
