# Research Notes: Page 123 — AI in Pakistan

This document records the research findings and methodological decisions for **Page 123: AI in Pakistan**.

## 1. Institutional Framework: NCAI
- **Role:** The National Centre of Artificial Intelligence (NCAI), based at NUST Islamabad, serves as the primary hub for AI research and commercialization in Pakistan.
- **Labs:** Nine core labs across six universities, including:
    - Intelligent Field Robotics Lab (NUST)
    - Smart City Lab (NED UET)
    - Medical Imaging & Diagnostics Lab (COMSATS)
    - Neuro-computation Lab (NED UET)
- **Output:** NCAI has developed 221 AI products and designs as of 2026, targeting sectors like agriculture, healthcare, and smart cities.

## 2. Policy & Sovereignty
- **Islamabad AI Declaration (9 February 2026):** A landmark policy document establishing Pakistan's commitment to "sovereign, responsible, and capability-driven AI."
- **Focus:** Emphasizes national productivity, institutional strength, public value, and ethical safeguards.
- **Shift:** Moves the conversation from mere adoption of foreign AI tools to building local capability and research capacity.

## 3. Economic Impact & Exports
- **IT Exports:** Reached $3.39 billion in the first nine months of FY 2025-26.
- **Freelance Economy:** Record earnings of $1.76 billion in FY 2026, with a growing shift toward AI-enabled services and automation.
- **Strategy:** Transitioning from a "Service Economy" (exporting hours) to a "Product Economy" (exporting AI-driven software products).

## 4. Local Language & Heritage
- **Pashto AI:** Emergence of local-language models like 'Qehwa AI' and 'Katib' (Peshawar students) addressing low-resource language gaps.
- **Orakzai Connection:** Potential for AI to bridge oral traditions and digital archives through automated transcription, translation, and metadata generation.

## 5. Young Pakistani Builders
- **Demographics:** Leveraging the "youth bulge" by providing access to AI tools, mathematics, and global markets.
- **Case Study: Faisal Orakzai:** Represented as a member of the broader generation of technologists growing up during the transition to AI-enabled computing.
- **Skills:** Emphasis on computer science fundamentals (math, statistics, systems) over simple prompt engineering.

## 6. Challenges & Constraints
- **Infrastructure:** Compute access (GPUs), electricity reliability, and research funding remain significant barriers.
- **Commercialization:** The need for better university-industry bridges to move research from labs to global markets.
- **Governance:** Balancing innovation with transparency, accountability, and human oversight.
