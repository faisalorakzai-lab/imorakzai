# Research Notes: Page 110 — Digital Heritage

This document records the research findings and methodological decisions for **Page 110: Digital Heritage**.

## 1. Digital Heritage Definition and Framework
- **Definition:** Digital heritage consists of unique resources of human knowledge and expression, including cultural, educational, scientific, and administrative materials, created digitally or converted from physical form (UNESCO Charter, 2003).
- **Core Pillars:** 
    - **Born-Digital:** Material created in digital format (social media, websites, digital photography).
    - **Digitized:** Material converted from physical to digital (scanned photos, digitized manuscripts).
    - **Preservation:** Ensuring long-term accessibility, authenticity, and usability.

## 2. Standards and Best Practices
- **UNESCO Charter (2003):** Emphasizes that digital heritage is at risk of being lost due to rapid obsolescence of hardware and software.
- **OAIS Reference Model (ISO 14721):** The standard framework for digital preservation systems (Ingest, Storage, Data Management, Administration, Preservation Planning, Access).
- **Metadata Standards:** 
    - **Dublin Core:** For descriptive metadata.
    - **PREMIS:** For preservation metadata (provenance, authenticity, fixity).
    - **IIIF:** For interoperable image viewing.
- **3-2-1 Strategy:** 3 copies, 2 different media types, 1 offsite location.

## 3. Indigenous and Community Archiving
- **Ethics:** Cultural sovereignty and informed consent are paramount. Indigenous communities (including Pashtun/Orakzai) should govern how their heritage is shared.
- **Sensitivity:** Distinction between public, community-only, and private/sacred materials.
- **Mukurtu Model:** A platform designed for indigenous communities to manage and share digital heritage according to their own protocols.

## 4. AI and Ethics in Heritage
- **Role of AI:** Assistant for transcription, translation, OCR, and metadata tagging.
- **Risks:** Fabrication of history ("hallucinations"), loss of cultural nuance, and lack of transparency.
- **Policy:** AI-generated content must be clearly labeled to maintain the integrity of historical evidence.

## 5. Orakzai Context
- **Heritage Types:** Family photos, oral histories, Pashto manuscripts, tribal genealogies, maps, and community records.
- **Vulnerability:** High risk due to displacement, conflict, and lack of institutional digital infrastructure.
- **Proposed Model:** An Orakzai Digital Heritage Archive that balances technological rigor with community humility and ethical governance.
