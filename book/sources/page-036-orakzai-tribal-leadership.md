# Sources: Page 36 — Orakzai Tribal Leadership

This document records the academic and historical sources supporting **Page 36: Orakzai Tribal Leadership** in *I’M ORAKZAI*.

## Primary References & Academic Citations

1. **King, Lucas White**: *Monograph on the Orakzai Country and Clans*.
   - **Publication**: Punjab Government Press, 1900.
   - **Details**: Documents the role of tribal elders and Maliks in the late 19th century, providing specific names of influential figures and the functioning of jirgas during colonial frontier administration.
   - **Claim Supported**: Traditional role of Maliks and elders as intermediaries; functioning of the jirga in Orakzai society.

2. **Khan, Sarfraz and Noor Ul Amin**: *Demystifying Tribal Autonomous Governance: A Critical Appraisal of Maliki System in Federally Administered Tribal Areas (FATA)*.
   - **Publication**: *Central Asia*, Vol. 76, No. 1, 2015.
   - **Details**: Academic analysis of the historical development and institutionalization of the Maliki system, arguing that colonial rule formalized and transformed traditional leadership into an administrative intermediary role.
   - **Claim Supported**: Colonial institutionalization of the Maliki system; distinction between traditional social authority and administratively recognized Maliki.

3. **Sammon, Robert L.**: *Mullas and Maliks: Evolution of the FATA and the Maliki System*.
   - **Publication**: Lauder Institute, University of Pennsylvania, 2008.
   - **Details**: Explores the duality of power between traditional tribal leaders (Maliks) and religious figures (Mullahs) in the tribal areas.
   - **Claim Supported**: Overlap and tension between religious and tribal leadership; evolution of governance in FATA.

4. **Constitution of Pakistan (25th Amendment)**: *FATA-KP Merger*.
   - **Year**: 2018.
   - **Details**: Legal instrument that merged the Federally Administered Tribal Areas (FATA) into Khyber Pakhtunkhwa (KP), ending the specialized FCR governance model.
   - **Claim Supported**: Transition to modern constitutional governance; end of the Political Agent system.

5. **Alam, J.**: *Jirga System and Its Role in Peacebuilding and Conflict Resolution*.
   - **Publication**: *Journal of South Asian Studies*, 2021.
   - **Details**: Discusses the procedural aspects of the jirga and its evolution from a purely customary institution to one recognized under colonial regulations.
   - **Claim Supported**: Jirga as a collective decision-making process; procedural stages of deliberation.

## Research Notes on Leadership Institutions

- **Traditional Elder**: Authority based on age, reputation, and consensus.
- **Malik**: Historically a community representative, later formalized as a state intermediary with stipends.
- **Political Agent**: A state-appointed official (not a tribal member) who held supreme administrative and judicial power in an Agency under the FCR.
- **Modern Governance**: Since 2018, Orakzai is a district of KP with elected representatives in the Provincial and National Assemblies.
