# Research Notes: Page 135 — Decentralized Finance (DeFi)

This document records the research findings and methodological decisions for **Page 135: Decentralized Finance (DeFi)**.

## 1. Conceptual Framework (2026)
- **Definition:** DeFi is an ecosystem of blockchain-based applications providing financial functions (lending, borrowing, trading) through smart contracts.
- **Core Shift:** Moving financial logic from institutional policies and employees to programmable, automated execution in code.
- **Key Characteristics:** Open access, non-custodial design, transparency, and composability ("Money Legos").
- **Interconnected Risk:** The benefit of composability creates systemic risks where failures can propagate through the ecosystem (e.g., oracle failure affecting lending protocols).

## 2. Institutional Landscape (Pakistan 2026)
- **Virtual Assets Act 2026:** Formally enacted by the Parliament of Pakistan. It establishes a comprehensive regulatory framework for cryptocurrencies, stablecoins, and tokenized assets.
- **SBP & PVARA Alignment:** The State Bank of Pakistan (SBP) and the Pakistan Virtual Assets Regulatory Authority (PVARA) are coordinating to regulate the $20-$25 billion digital currency market held by Pakistanis.
- **Financial Inclusion Paradigm:** Research from MDPI and LinkedIn (2026) highlights DeFi's role in providing sustainable digital payment systems and lowering remittance costs for the Pakistani diaspora.
- **Market Maturity:** Moving from a "legal grey zone" to a regulated environment, facilitating institutional adoption and asset tokenization.

## 3. Technical Components
- **Automated Market Makers (AMMs):** Mathematical formulas and liquidity pools replacing traditional order books.
- **Overcollateralization:** A necessary buffer against crypto-asset volatility in lending protocols.
- **Oracles:** The critical bridge between blockchain state and external data (prices, interest rates).
- **Flash Loans:** High-speed financial tools that allow borrowing without collateral within a single transaction, serving as a testing ground for economic security.

## 4. Orakzai & Regional Context
- **Remittance Friction:** How DeFi protocols can reduce layers in the international payment process for the Orakzai diaspora.
- **Access:** Digital wallets providing remote communities with access to global liquidity and programmable savings products without traditional bank accounts.

- **Philosophy:** "Blockchain is not merely an asset class, but infrastructure for programmable financial systems."
