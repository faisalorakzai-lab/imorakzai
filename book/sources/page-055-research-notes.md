# Research Notes: Page 55 — Turban & Cultural Identity

## Core Concept
- **Turban as Language**: A piece of cloth that communicates weather protection, social status, and identity.
- **Layered History**: Belongs to both a wider regional history (South/Central Asia) and a specific Pashtun cultural world.

## Key Findings

### 1. Terminology
- **Paṭkay (پټکی)**: The most common Pashto term for a turban. Used in everyday speech.
- **Dastār (دستار)**: A formal, often literary or religious term for a turban. Associated with leadership and scholarship.
- **Pagṛi (پګړۍ)**: A regional term used across South Asia, also common in Pashto-speaking areas.
- **Langōṭā / Lungi**: Often refers to the long piece of cloth used for wrapping, or a specific decorated variety.

### 2. Wrapping as Identity
- **Visual Form**: The way the cloth is wrapped creates distinct silhouettes associated with different regions or tribes.
- **Components**: Often includes a **Kulla** (under-cap) around which the cloth is wound. The **Shamla** (the loose end standing up or hanging down) is a key stylistic feature.
- **Regional Styles**:
    - **Afridi/Wazir style**: Often high-pointed or voluminous.
    - **Yousafzai style**: Typically more compact or low-wrapped.
    - **Orakzai context**: Men in the Tirah region historically used styles suited for mountain life—practical yet distinct.

### 3. Social and Cultural Meaning
- **Elders and Status**: Turbans are strongly associated with maturity, wisdom, and authority. Wrapping a turban on a younger man is a ceremony of transition (*Dastār Bandi*).
- **Honour (Nang) and Dignity (Ghairat)**: The turban is a physical manifestation of a man's social standing. To "knock off a turban" is a grave insult.
- **Jirga**: In formal tribal gatherings, the turban signifies the participant's role as a representative of his family or clan.

### 4. Materials and Environment
- **Cotton**: The most common material, often white or indigo-dyed.
- **Wool**: Used in winter for warmth.
- **Silk**: Reserved for ceremonial occasions (weddings, leadership ceremonies).
- **Climate Function**: Protects against sun, dust, cold, and wind in the varied Orakzai terrain.

### 5. Contemporary Change
- **Urbanization**: In cities like Kohat or Peshawar, the turban is often replaced by the *Pakol* or Western-style headwear for daily use, becoming a ceremonial item.
- **Diaspora**: Used as a symbol of "Pashtunness" during cultural festivals and family weddings.

## Evidence Hierarchy
- **A. Orakzai-Specific**: Historical photographs of Tirah tribesmen; local terminology recorded in ethnographic accounts.
- **B. Pashtun-Wide**: *Patkay* terminology; *Dastār Bandi* ceremonies; *Nang* associations.
- **C. Regional**: *Kulla* usage; cotton/silk material traditions.
- **D. Historical**: British Raj army records (Afridi/Orakzai battalions); historical travelogues.
