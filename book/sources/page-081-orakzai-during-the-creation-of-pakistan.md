# Sources: Page 81 — Orakzai During the Creation of Pakistan

This document records the primary, secondary, and administrative sources supporting **Page 81: Orakzai During the Creation of Pakistan** in *I’M ORAKZAI*.

## Constitutional & Legal Records

1. **United Kingdom Parliament.** (1947). *Indian Independence Act 1947*.
   - **Details**: Section 7(1)(c) specifies the lapsing of treaties and agreements with tribal areas.
   - **Claim Supported**: Termination of British paramountcy.

2. **Government of Pakistan.** (1947-1948). *Tribal Agreements (Jirga Records)*.
   - **Details**: Documents signed between the new state of Pakistan and various tribal jirgas to maintain administrative continuity.
   - **Claim Supported**: Negotiated transition to Pakistan.

## Academic & Historical Studies

3. **Khan, I.** (2015). "Special Status of Tribal Areas (FATA): An Artificial Imperial Construct." *Slavic-Eurasian Research Center*.
   - **Details**: Analysis of the administrative status of Orakzai and other agencies during the 1947 transition.
   - **Claim Supported**: Orakzai's status within FR Kohat and the legacy of the FCR.

4. **Ahmad, M. S., Memon, A., & Rabbi, F.** (2014). "Electoral Politics in the North West Frontier Province of Colonial India 1946–47." *History and Sociology of South Asia*.
   - **Details**: Detailed account of the 1947 referendum and the political mobilization in the NWFP.
   - **Claim Supported**: Scope and limitations of the 1947 referendum.

5. **Cheema, M., & Yousaf, F.** (2020). "Constitutionalizing a perpetual transition: The 'integration' of the Pashtun 'tribal areas' in Pakistan." *International Journal of Constitutional Law*.
   - **Details**: Legal analysis of the semi-autonomous status of tribal areas after 1947.
   - **Claim Supported**: Post-independence administrative continuity.

6. **Shah, S. W. A.** (2007). *North-West Frontier Province: History and Politics*. NIHCR.
   - **Details**: Comprehensive history of the regional political movements leading to 1947.
   - **Claim Supported**: Regional Pashtun political context.
