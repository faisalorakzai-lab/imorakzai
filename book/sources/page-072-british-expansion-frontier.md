# Sources: Page 72 — British Expansion in the Frontier

This document records the historical and academic sources supporting **Page 72: British Expansion in the Frontier** in *I’M ORAKZAI*.

## Primary References & Academic Citations

1. **Agha, Sameetah.** (2021). "The Tirah Campaign, 1897–1898". *Queen Victoria's Wars: British Military Campaigns, 1857–1902*. Cambridge University Press.
   - **Details**: Comprehensive analysis of the major 1897 campaign involving Orakzai and Afridi tribes.
   - **Claim Supported**: Orakzai involvement in the 1897 uprising and the scale of the British military response.

2. **Tripodi, Christian.** (2013). *Edge of Empire: The British Political Officer and Tribal Administration on the North-West Frontier 1877–1947*. Ashgate Publishing.
   - **Details**: Academic study of the Political Agent system and the mechanisms of indirect rule.
   - **Claim Supported**: Role of Political Agents, tribal allowances, and the system of patronage.

3. **Moreman, T. R.** (1998). *The Army in India and the Development of Frontier Warfare, 1849–1947*. Macmillan.
   - **Details**: Military history of frontier operations, specifically the 1891 Miranzai campaign.
   - **Claim Supported**: Details of the 1891 expedition targeting the Samana Range and Orakzai clans.

4. **Khan, Rashid.** (2020). "Geopolitics and Strategic Interests of the British in the Tribal Region". *Central Asia Journal*.
   - **Details**: Exploration of the strategic motivations behind British frontier expansion.
   - **Claim Supported**: Definition of "The Frontier" as a security buffer and administrative edge.

5. **Hutchinson, H. D.** (1898). *The Campaign in Tirah, 1897-1898: An Account of the Expedition Against the Orakzais and Afridis*. London: Macmillan.
   - **Details**: Contemporary British account of the Tirah Expedition.
   - **Claim Supported**: Direct evidence of military engagements and tribal resistance in Orakzai territory.
