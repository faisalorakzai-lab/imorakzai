# Research Notes: Page 90 — Orakzai Diaspora

## Core Concept
- **The Living Social Network**: The Orakzai diaspora is a trans-local community maintained through movement, memory, and social ties.
- **Not One Experience**: Diaspora formation is driven by diverse factors: conflict displacement, economic necessity, educational aspiration, and family reunification.
- **Distance vs. Belonging**: Physical distance does not erase cultural belonging; identity is carried and adapted in new urban and international contexts.

## Key Migration & Displacement Data

### 1. Conflict-Induced Displacement (2009–2011)
- **UNHCR (Dec 2009)**: Estimated **40,000 people** (approx. 2,400 families) fled Orakzai Agency to neighbouring **Hangu District** as military operations intensified.
- **IDMC (2010)**: Reported **190,000 people** displaced from Bajaur, Mohmand, Khyber, and Orakzai Agencies in 2009.
- **UNHCR (2011)**: Reported that approximately **23,000 households** displaced from Orakzai had returned by the end of 2010.
- **Note**: Displacement often relied on kinship networks rather than formal camps, with many staying with host families in Hangu and Kohat.

### 2. Urban Settlement Hubs
- **Karachi**: Major Orakzai concentrations in **Sohrab Goth**, **Banaras**, and **Pathan Colony**. Karachi serves as the primary economic hub for Pashtun migrants, with heavy involvement in the transport and retail sectors.
- **Peshawar**: A center for education, healthcare, and professional settlement. Peshawar acted as a "sanctuary city" for Orakzai families during periods of instability.
- **Hangu & Kohat**: Primary destinations due to geographic contiguity. Orakzai tribesmen have historically purchased property and established businesses in the **Miranzai Valley** (Hangu/Thall/Doaba).
- **Islamabad/Rawalpindi**: Growing hub for Orakzai students, government employees, and professionals.

### 3. International Diaspora
- **Gulf Region (UAE, KSA)**: The most significant destination for labor migration. Remittances from the Gulf are a cornerstone of the Orakzai rural economy.
- **Global Professional Diaspora**: Orakzai individuals are increasingly present in the **United Kingdom**, **Europe**, and **North America**, particularly in professional fields (medicine, engineering, academia).

## Diaspora Classification Matrix

| Type | Driver | Geographic Scope | Nature |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Internal (Regional)** | Proximity, Kinship | Hangu, Kohat, Peshawar | Permanent/Circular |
| **Internal (National)** | Economy, Security | Karachi, Islamabad | Long-term/Settled |
| **Conflict-Induced** | Military Operations | Neighboring Districts | Forced/Temporary |
| **International (Labor)** | Remittances | UAE, Saudi Arabia | Circular/Contract |
| **International (Prof.)** | Education, Career | UK, USA, Europe | Permanent/Professional |

## Identity Maintenance in Diaspora
- **Language**: Pashto remains the primary domestic language in diaspora households.
- **Social Networks**: Tribal and clan-based associations (often informal) facilitate job placement, housing, and social support in cities like Karachi.
- **Digital Connectivity**: Mobile networks and social media (WhatsApp, Facebook) bridge the gap between the highland village and the global city, allowing for "real-time" participation in tribal life.

## Author's Reflection Themes
- **The Collection of Journeys**: Every migrant family carries a unique story of adaptation and resilience.
- **The Anchor of Home**: The ancestral village remains the psychological reference point, even for those born in the city.
- **Trans-local Future**: The Orakzai community is no longer confined to its mountains; it is a global network of people who carry the mountain perspective with them.
