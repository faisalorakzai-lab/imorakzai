# Research Notes: Page 117 — The Pakistani Startup Ecosystem

This document records the research findings and methodological decisions for **Page 117: The Pakistani Startup Ecosystem**.

## 1. Ecosystem Definition & Framework
- **Startup vs. Small Business:** Startups are defined by their repeatable and scalable models, often leveraging technology to solve problems under high uncertainty.
- **Ecosystem Components:** A network including founders, employees, customers, capital (VC/Angel), universities, government (Ignite/PSF), infrastructure, and mentors.
- **Evolution:** The transition from service-based software houses to product-oriented startups, fueled by mobile broadband and digital payment infrastructure.

## 2. Funding Dynamics (2025–2026)
- **2025 Performance:** Disclosed equity funding was approximately **US$36.6 million** (Data Darbar), while hybrid models (equity + debt) pushed the total to **US$74.2 million** (Invest2Innovate/Kalsoom Lakhani).
- **2026 Surge:** By July 2026, equity funding reached **US$113 million** across 9 rounds, representing a 371% increase compared to the previous year, signaling a rebound in investor confidence.
- **Funding Stages:** Angel → Pre-Seed → Seed → Series A → Growth.
- **Structural Shift:** Increased focus on sustainable revenue models over pure growth, with a shift toward SaaS, Fintech, and AI-driven solutions.

## 3. Institutional & Regulatory Support
- **Ignite / Pakistan Startup Fund (PSF):** Catalyzing private and foreign investment through non-equity grants and cloud infrastructure support.
- **National Incubation Centers (NIC 2.0):** Expanded network including new centers (e.g., NIC Sialkot) and enhanced services through partners like Founder Institute (NIC Islamabad).
- **Regulators:** SECP (digital company registration) and SBP (Fintech regulation, Raast, and digital banking licenses).
- **Inclusion:** Specific initiatives for women-led businesses (e.g., Standard Chartered WIN loans up to $150k) and youth-led startups.

## 4. Sector Analysis
- **Fintech:** Driven by mobile broadband and the Raast payment system; focus on digital wallets, lending, and wealth tech.
- **E-commerce:** Moving toward social commerce and direct-to-consumer (D2C) brands.
- **Healthtech & Edtech:** Leveraging telemedicine and digital learning platforms to bridge geographic gaps.
- **AI & SaaS:** Emerging wave of startups using AI for automation, translation, and data analysis, with a focus on global markets.

## 5. Case Study: Faisal Orakzai
- **Profile:** Contemporary technology entrepreneur oriented toward digital infrastructure and international markets.
- **Projects:** Documented associations with **Orakzai Group**, **OkzByte Hub**, **Orakzai Bond**, and **Shamim Forever**.
- **Context:** Illustrates the "Global Mindset" where Orakzai identity and technical expertise (Computer Science/Blockchain/AI) converge to build digital products.
- **Labeling:** Identified as an individual case study, not a statistical representation of the Orakzai community.

## 6. Structural Challenges & Gaps
- **Challenges:** Macroeconomic instability, brain drain, limited exit opportunities (IPOs/Acquisitions), and regulatory fragmentation.
- **Research Gaps:** Startup histories outside major hubs (Karachi/Lahore/ISB), tribal-district entrepreneurship, and long-term impact of diaspora networks.
