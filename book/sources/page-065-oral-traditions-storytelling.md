# Sources: Page 65 — Oral Traditions & Storytelling

This document records the academic and ethnographic sources supporting **Page 65: Oral Traditions & Storytelling** in *I’M ORAKZAI*.

## Primary References & Academic Citations

1. **Kreyenbroek, P. G. & Marzolph, U.** (2010). *Oral Literature of Iranian Languages: Kurdish, Pashto, Balochi, Ossetic, Persian and Tajik*. I.B. Tauris.
   - **Details**: Contains a collection of Pashto folk stories, with the first four stories specifically from the Orakzai dialect group.
   - **Claim Supported**: Existence of a documented Orakzai-specific oral storytelling corpus.

2. **Kushev, V. V.** (1997). "Areal Lexical Contacts of the Afghan (Pashto) Language". *JSTOR*.
   - **Details**: Mentions Ali Akbar Orakzai as a 17th-century poet and scholar whose work *Gulshan-i Afghan* is a key literary and oral milestone.
   - **Claim Supported**: Ali Akbar Orakzai's contribution to Pashto satire and literature.

3. **Naseer, N.** (2026). "Early Warning by Women for Conflict Prevention in the Erstwhile Federally Administered Tribal Areas". *CSQ Journal*.
   - **Details**: Fieldwork documenting women's oral narratives in Orakzai and Kurram.
   - **Claim Supported**: Women's role as carriers of oral tradition and community safety knowledge.

4. **Tair, M. N. & Edwards, L.** (2009). *Rohi Mataluna: Pashto Proverbs*. Resource Publications.
   - **Details**: Comprehensive collection of proverbs used in storytelling and daily speech.
   - **Claim Supported**: Function of proverbs within the oral tradition.

5. **IM Sciences**. (2021). "Preservation and Dissemination of Indigenous Knowledge in Pashtuns' Culture: The Role of Hujra as a Learning Hub".
   - **Details**: Research on the Hujra as a center for oral transmission.
   - **Claim Supported**: Hujra as a social setting for storytelling.
