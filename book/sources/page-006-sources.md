# Page 6 Sources: The Meaning of Orakzai

### 1. Orakzai as a Pashtun/Pathan Tribal Community
- **Author**: Encyclopaedia Britannica (11th Edition)
- **Title**: Orakzai
- **Publication**: Encyclopaedia Britannica
- **Publisher**: Cambridge University Press
- **Year**: 1911
- **Source Type**: Historical Reference Work
- **What claim the source supports**: Identifies Orakzai as a Pathan tribe of the North-West Frontier.

### 2. Historical Geographic Association with Tirah
- **Author**: L. W. King
- **Title**: The Orakzai Country and Clans
- **Publication**: Monograph
- **Publisher**: Punjab Government Press (Original), Vanguard Books (Reprint)
- **Year**: 1900 (Original), 1984 (Reprint)
- **Source Type**: Historical Monograph / Academic Book
- **What claim the source supports**: Documents the geographic location of Orakzai clans in Tirah and the Mastura Valley.

### 3. The Traditional "Lost Son" Interpretation
- **Author**: Haroon Rashid
- **Title**: History of the Pathans (Vol. IV)
- **Publisher**: Haroon Rashid
- **Year**: 2002
- **Page number**: 52
- **Source Type**: Scholarly Work on Pashtun Tribal History
- **What claim the source supports**: Records the literal meaning of Orakzai as "lost son" (Wrak Zoy).

### 4. The Sikandar Shah Origin Tradition
- **Author**: White King (L. W. King)
- **Title**: The Orakzai Country and Clans
- **Publisher**: Punjab Government Press
- **Year**: 1900
- **Source Type**: Ethnographic Study / Colonial Record
- **What claim the source supports**: Describes the tradition of Sikandar Shah, the Persian Prince, as the ancestor of the Orakzai.

### 5. Historical Variation in Spelling/Transliteration
- **Author**: H. G. Raverty
- **Title**: Notes on Afghanistan and Part of Baluchistan
- **Publisher**: Eyre and Spottiswoode
- **Year**: 1888
- **Source Type**: Historical Text / Geographical Notes
- **What claim the source supports**: Demonstrates various transliterations of Pashto names and tribal designations in 19th-century English records.
