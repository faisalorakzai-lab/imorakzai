# Page 100 Research Notes — The Modern Orakzai

## Scope and Methodology

Page 100 serves as a milestone, bringing the first 100 pages of the book into the present. It explores what it means to be Orakzai in a world defined by the 2018 FATA merger, digital transformation, and global connectivity. The methodology emphasizes the coexistence of tradition and modernity, using verified contemporary evidence to ground its claims.

Research focuses on:
1.  **Administrative Evolution**: The transition from Orakzai Agency to Orakzai District post-2018 merger.
2.  **Digital Skills and Youth Development**: Using the 2025 UNDP/Japan digital skills initiative as a primary evidence anchor.
3.  **Career Aspirations**: Documenting contemporary youth dialogues through the 2025 Orakzai District Youth Office career counselling seminars.
4.  **The Modern Identity Formula**: Integrating education, technology, and global connection with traditional tribal roots.

## Key Research Findings

### 1. Administrative Integration (Post-2018)
- **Status**: Orakzai is now a district of Khyber Pakhtunkhwa.
- **Evidence**: The 25th Amendment to the Constitution of Pakistan (2018) formally merged FATA into KP.
- **Impact**: Shift from tribal administration to provincial laws, policing, and judicial systems.
- **Constraint**: Administrative change is not treated as cultural disappearance; tribal identity persists within the new civic framework.

### 2. Digital Transformation (2025 Evidence)
- **Programme**: UNDP/Japan-supported digital skills initiative.
- **Scope**: 600 young people from Orakzai and Kurram.
- **Demographics**: 52% women participation.
- **Skills**: Freelancing, software, and digital marketing.
- **Classification**: Contemporary Development Evidence. This demonstrates a specific pathway for modern Orakzai youth to access the global market.

### 3. Contemporary Youth Dialogue
- **Event**: 2025 District Youth Office Orakzai career counselling seminar.
- **Themes**: Education, legal paths to earning, peacebuilding, and community development.
- **Significance**: Shows that modern Orakzai youth are actively discussing careers in the knowledge economy while maintaining a commitment to their local community.

### 4. Intergenerational Continuity
- **Concept**: Modernity is not Westernization.
- **Finding**: Scholarship (e.g., Shinwari, 2025) highlights that young Pashtuns, including Orakzai, navigate between tribal elders' values and modern education/technology.
- **Dialogue**: The "Elder → History" and "Youth → Technology" exchange creates a living heritage rather than a fossilized culture.

## Research Gaps and Limitations
- **District-Wide Internet Penetration**: No verified 2026 percentage for total household internet access in Orakzai District.
- **Post-Merger Economic Data**: Limited longitudinal data on the specific economic impact of the merger on Orakzai's traditional trades.
- **Aspiration Surveys**: No large-scale, Orakzai-specific survey on future career preferences beyond individual seminar records.

## Evidence Cautions

The UNDP figure of 600 trainees and 52% women is a programme-specific count for participants from Orakzai and Kurram in a 2025 initiative. It is not an estimate of all Orakzai youth, all Orakzai women, district-wide digital access, or district-wide employment. The youth career-counselling record documents a public event and its themes; it does not establish the aspirations of every young person. The 2018 merger establishes administrative change, not the disappearance of tribal, family, linguistic, religious, or cultural identity.
