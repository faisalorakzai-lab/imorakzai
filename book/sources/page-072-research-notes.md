# Research Notes: Page 72 — British Expansion in the Frontier

## Core Concept
- **Frontier as a Process**: British expansion into the Orakzai frontier was a gradual, contested, and uneven process beginning with the 1849 annexation of Punjab and intensifying in the late 19th century.
- **Administrative Transition**: The shift from Sikh-era influence to formal British frontier policy involved a combination of direct military pressure and indirect political management through Political Agents and tribal intermediaries.
- **Key Historical Markers**:
    - **1849**: Annexation of Punjab; British reach the Kohat border.
    - **1855, 1868, 1869**: Early punitive expeditions against Orakzai sections.
    - **1891**: The Miranzai Expedition (General Sir William Lockhart) targeting the Samana Range and Orakzai clans.
    - **1897-1898**: The Tirah Expedition; the largest colonial military deployment on the frontier to date, involving Orakzai and Afridi resistance.

## Key Findings

### 1. The Meaning of "The Frontier"
- **British Perspective**: A security boundary, administrative edge, and political buffer against Russian/Afghan influence.
- **Local Perspective**: A homeland, village network, and ancestral grazing land (Tirah).
- **Source**: *Geopolitics and Strategic Interests of the British in the Tribal Region* (R. Khan, 2020).

### 2. Annexation of Punjab (1849)
- The fall of the Sikh Empire in 1849 led to the British inheritance of the frontier districts (Kohat, Peshawar).
- This did not mean immediate control over the tribal hills, but rather a new "Close Border Policy" where the British attempted to manage the border from the plains.
- **Source**: *The North-West Frontier* (National Army Museum).

### 3. Indirect Rule and Political Agents
- **Political Agents**: Acted as the primary link between the state and the tribes, using negotiation, intelligence, and subsidies to manage the frontier.
- **Allowances**: Documented payments to Maliks and tribal sections to ensure the safety of roads and passes, though these did not guarantee loyalty.
- **Source**: *Edge of Empire: The British Political Officer and Tribal Administration* (C. Tripodi, 2013).

### 4. Infrastructure and Control
- **Roads**: Served a dual role of connectivity (trade) and control (military access).
- **Forts**: Key posts on the Samana Range included Fort Lockhart and Fort Gulistan, which served as eyes over the Orakzai valleys.
- **Source**: *The Army in India and Mountain Warfare* (T.R. Moreman, 1998).

### 5. Orakzai-Specific Military Campaigns
- **1855 Expedition**: Punitive action following border raids.
- **1891 Miranzai Expedition**: Aimed at securing the Samana Range; involved fierce fighting with Orakzai clans.
- **1897 Tirah Campaign**: Triggered by a widespread tribal uprising; General Sir William Lockhart led 34,000 troops into the heart of Orakzai and Afridi territory.
- **Battle of Saragarhi (1897)**: A significant engagement where Orakzai and Afridi tribesmen attacked a British signal post.
- **Source**: *The Campaign in Tirah, 1897-1898* (H.D. Hutchinson, 1898); *Queen Victoria's Wars* (S. Agha, 2021).

## Evidence Hierarchy
- **A. Historical Records**: British Parliamentary papers, India Office records, military despatches.
- **B. Orakzai-Specific**: 1891 and 1897 campaign records; reports on Orakzai clans by L. White King.
- **C. Regional**: Punjab administration reports; North-West Frontier Province gazetteers.
- **D. Contemporary Scholarship**: Academic studies on colonial frontier management and tribal resistance.

## Documented Terminology
- **Forward Policy**: The late 19th-century shift toward direct intervention in the tribal hills.
- **Punitive Expedition**: Military action taken as punishment for tribal raids or breaches of agreement.
- **Malik**: A tribal leader recognized by the British for administrative negotiation.
- **Political Agency**: The administrative unit used to manage tribal territories indirectly.
- **Samana Range**: The strategic mountain ridge overlooking Orakzai territory.
