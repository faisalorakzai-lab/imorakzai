# Sources: Page 53 — Orakzai Hospitality

This document records the academic and historical sources supporting **Page 53: Orakzai Hospitality** in *I’M ORAKZAI*.

## Primary References & Academic Citations

1. **Ahmed, Akbar S.**: *Pukhtun Economy and Society: Traditional Structure and Economic Development in a Tribal Society*. Routledge (1980).
   - **Details**: Comprehensive ethnographic study of Pashtun tribal institutions, including Melmastia and the Hujra.
   - **Claim Supported**: Melmastia as a pillar of Pashtunwali and its social implications.

2. **Ullah, J.**: *Militancy and Pashtun Culture: Challenges and Developments in Pashtun Society in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, Pakistan*. LASSIJ (2019).
   - **Details**: Research on how security constraints have affected traditional social practices like hospitality in former FATA and KP.
   - **Claim Supported**: Impact of militancy and insecurity on traditional Melmastia.

3. **Infinity Press / Journal of Social Sciences**: *Role of Malik in Pukhtoon tribal areas*.
   - **Details**: Case study focused on the village of Mirbak in Orakzai Agency, documenting the Malik's responsibility to maintain a Hujra and provide hospitality.
   - **Claim Supported**: Orakzai-specific Hujra and hospitality responsibilities.

4. **Bukhari, A. Q.**: *Layered Continuity in Pakistan's Northwest Borderlands: State, Power, and Political Economy*. (2026).
   - **Details**: Analysis of political economy and social continuity in the borderlands, mentioning Orakzai leadership and hospitality.
   - **Claim Supported**: Continuity of hospitality practices in the Orakzai context.

5. **Encyclopaedia Iranica**: *Hospitality (Melmastia)*.
   - **Details**: Authoritative entry on the cultural and linguistic roots of Melmastia across the Pashtun world.
   - **Claim Supported**: General Pashtun-wide principles of hospitality.

## Documented Social Frameworks

| Concept | Cultural Meaning | Documented Role |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Melmastia** | Hospitality | A moral obligation to receive and protect guests. |
| **Hujra** | Community Space | The physical venue for public hospitality and social exchange. |
| **Dastarkhan** | Shared Space | The ritual of sharing food as a bond of peace and recognition. |
| **Nang/Ghairat** | Honor/Dignity | The underlying values that mandate the treatment of a guest. |
