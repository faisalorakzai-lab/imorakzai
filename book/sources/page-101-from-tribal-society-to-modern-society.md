# Sources: Page 101 — From Tribal Society to Modern Society

This source record documents the evidence used for **Page 101: From Tribal Society to Modern Society** in *I’M ORAKZAI*. The page analyzes the transition from traditional community structures to modern institutional life.

## Source 1 — Ethnographic Foundation

**Author:** Akbar S. Ahmed  
**Title:** *Pukhtun economy and society: Traditional structure and economic development in a tribal society*  
**Publisher:** Routledge (Original 1980; 2011 Edition)  
**Claim supported:** Provides foundational social analysis of Orakzai tribal structure, lineage, and political administration based on fieldwork in Orakzai.  
**Evidence level:** Foundational academic scholarship.

## Source 2 — Constitutional Transition

**Author:** Government of Pakistan  
**Title:** *Constitution (Twenty-fifth Amendment) Act, 2018*  
**Publisher:** Gazette of Pakistan  
**Year:** 2018  
**Claim supported:** The formal merger of FATA into Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, ending the FCR era and integrating the region into the national constitutional framework.  
**Evidence level:** Official constitutional record.

## Source 3 — Vocational Training Evidence (2024)

**Institution:** UNDP Pakistan / Government of Japan  
**Programme:** Market-oriented vocational and technical skills training  
**Date:** November 2024  
**URL:** [ReliefWeb Report](https://reliefweb.int/report/pakistan/transformative-step-towards-resilient-khyber-pakhtunkhwa-700-young-people-have-acquired-market-driven-skills-through-vocational-training-supported-government-japan)  
**Claim supported:** 700 youth (including 375 women) from Kurram and Orakzai were trained in technical skills such as solar systems and mobile repair.  
**Evidence level:** Contemporary development evidence.

## Source 4 — Digital Skills Evidence (2025)

**Institution:** UNDP Pakistan / Government of Japan  
**Programme:** Digital skills training for youth  
**Date:** April 2025  
**URL:** [UNDP Press Release](https://www.undp.org/pakistan/press-releases/600-young-people-kurram-and-orakzai-trained-digital-skills-under-government-japan-undp-initiative)  
**Claim supported:** 600 young people from Kurram and Orakzai received digital training (52% women) focused on freelancing and remote work.  
**Evidence level:** Contemporary development evidence.

## Source 5 — Youth and Social Change

**Author:** Naveed Ahmad Shinwari  
**Title:** *Political and social change in Pakistan's tribal areas: Understanding Pashtun youth*  
**Year:** 2025  
**Claim supported:** Analysis of how young Pashtuns in merged districts navigate between tribal elders' values and modern education/technology.  
**Evidence level:** Academic scholarship.

## Evidence Boundary

- **No Generalization**: Training cohort numbers (e.g., 52% women) are not presented as district-wide employment or education rates.
- **No Erasure**: Legal and institutional changes are documented as shifts in the formal framework, not as proof of the disappearance of tribal identity.
- **Interpretation Labeled**: The "Digital Hujra" and "Adapted Hospitality" concepts are identified as analytical frameworks for understanding social change, not as pre-existing institutional labels.
- **Gaps Acknowledged**: The page explicitly states that research on village-level social change and the modern role of the hujra remains limited.
