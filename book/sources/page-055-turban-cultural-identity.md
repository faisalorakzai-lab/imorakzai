# Sources: Page 55 — Turban & Cultural Identity

This document records the academic and historical sources supporting **Page 54: Turban & Cultural Identity** in *I’M ORAKZAI*.

## Primary References & Academic Citations

1. **Encyclopaedia Iranica**: *Clothing xiii. Clothing in Afghanistan*.
   - **Details**: Authoritative entry on Pashtun headgear, documenting the use of white cotton, black cloth, and silk turbans among different groups.
   - **Claim Supported**: General Pashtun turban traditions, materials, and symbolic value.

2. **Dawar, S.**: *Continuity of Gandhara Culture in Pakistan: Some Selected Objects of Daily Life*. (2013).
   - **Details**: Research on the historical continuity of objects, specifically mentioning the *Kulla* (under-cap) of the Pashtun turban and its presence in the Orakzai Agency.
   - **Claim Supported**: Historical and regional use of the *Kulla* in Orakzai.

3. **Ahmed, A. S.**: *Pukhtun Economy and Society: Traditional Structure and Economic Development in a Tribal Society*. (1980).
   - **Details**: Classic ethnography of Pashtun tribal structure, discussing the social significance of the turban in leadership and Jirga.
   - **Claim Supported**: Turban's role in social status, authority, and tribal governance.

4. **Lindholm, C.**: *Frontier Perspectives: Essays in Comparative Anthropology*. (1996).
   - **Details**: Anthropological study of the North-West Frontier, examining the concepts of honour (*Nang*) and their visual expressions.
   - **Claim Supported**: Connection between headwear, dignity, and cultural identity.

5. **Historical Records of the Indian Army**: *Afridi and Orakzai Battalions*. (British Raj Era).
   - **Details**: Visual and descriptive records of different turban wrapping styles used to distinguish tribal groups in military service.
   - **Claim Supported**: Regional and tribal variations in turban wrapping.
