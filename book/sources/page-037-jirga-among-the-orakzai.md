# Sources: Page 37 — Jirga Among the Orakzai

This document records the academic and historical sources supporting **Page 37: Jirga Among the Orakzai** in *I’M ORAKZAI*.

## Primary References & Academic Citations

1. **King, Lucas White**: *Monograph on the Orakzai Country and Clans*.
   - **Publication**: Punjab Government Press, 1900.
   - **Details**: Documents the functioning of jirgas in the late 19th century, highlighting the role of tribal ethics and customary practices (Pashtunwali) in Orakzai society.
   - **Claim Supported**: Historical use of jirga for conflict resolution; role of elders and sections in deliberation.

2. **Alam, J.**: *Jirga System and Its Role in Peacebuilding and Conflict Resolution*.
   - **Publication**: *Journal of South Asian Studies*, 2021.
   - **Details**: Academic analysis of the jirga as a conflict resolution institution, focusing on its procedural stages and socio-cultural significance.
   - **Claim Supported**: Jirga as a deliberative process; stages of dialogue, deliberation, and reconciliation.

3. **Mahsud, N. H. K.**: *Frontier Crimes Regulation (FCR): From Introduction to Abolition*.
   - **Publication**: *Global Political Review*, 2021.
   - **Details**: Explains how the FCR regulated and transformed the jirga into a tool of colonial and post-colonial administration, granting Political Agents the power to refer cases to jirgas.
   - **Claim Supported**: Integration of jirga into the FCR legal framework; discretionary power of Political Agents.

4. **Constitution of Pakistan (25th Amendment)**: *FATA-KP Merger*.
   - **Year**: 2018.
   - **Details**: Legal instrument that abolished the FCR and integrated Orakzai into the mainstream judicial and constitutional system of Pakistan.
   - **Claim Supported**: Modern transformation of Orakzai governance; transition from FCR-based jirga to formal courts.

5. **Jirga System in Orakzai**: *Pashto Research Journal*.
   - **URL**: http://pashto.org.pk/index.php/path/article/view/500
   - **Details**: Specific research on the jirga institution within the Orakzai tribal context, discussing its role in resolving land, family, and interpersonal disputes.
   - **Claim Supported**: Orakzai-specific jirga traditions; categories of disputes handled by tribal councils.

## Research Notes on Jirga Evolution

- **Customary Jirga**: Operates based on social consensus and tribal standing.
- **FCR Jirga**: A regulated council used by the state to provide "findings of fact" to the Political Agent.
- **Modern Mediation**: Post-2018, jirgas function as informal community mediation bodies without formal judicial power.
- **Limitations**: Historical jirgas were male-dominated and did not always guarantee procedural fairness or protection for vulnerable groups.
