# Page 99 Research Notes — Identity in the Diaspora

## Scope and Methodology

Page 99 examines the multi-layered nature of Orakzai identity in diaspora settings. The methodology emphasizes that identity is not a fixed, singular label but a dynamic negotiation between heritage (homeland, family, language) and current reality (host society, profession, personal choice). 

Research focuses on:
1.  **Multi-layered Identity**: The intersection of tribal (Orakzai), ethnic (Pashtun), national (Pakistani/Host country), and religious (Muslim) identities.
2.  **Intergenerational Transmission**: How identity is passed down and reinterpreted by the second and third generations.
3.  **Language and Identity**: The role of Pashto as a cultural anchor vs. the pressures of multilingualism and language shift.
4.  **Digital Identity**: The use of social media and AI as contemporary sites for identity expression and preservation.

## Key Research Findings

### 1. The Multi-layered Self
- **Scholarship**: Research on British Pakistanis (e.g., Modood, 2005; Werbner, 2002) identifies "multiple belongings" where individuals navigate different identity spheres simultaneously.
- **Evidence Level**: General Diaspora / Pakistani Diaspora context.
- **Orakzai Application**: This scholarship provides a conceptual framework for multiple belonging; it does not prove how Orakzai individuals rank identity labels. Page 99 therefore presents possible combinations and asks people to describe their own priorities.

### 2. Tribal Identity in European Labor Contexts
- **Specific Finding**: A study of Pashtun food delivery workers in Milan, Italy, explicitly mentions the persistence of tribal factions (Bangash and Orakzai) in the diaspora. This demonstrates that tribal identity remains a relevant social and organizational category even in modern Western labor markets.
- **Evidence Level**: Orakzai-specific (Regional/Labor).

### 3. Intergenerational Negotiation
- **2nd/3rd Generation**: Rafiq and Dunnett’s qualitative study of three generations of British Pakistani Muslims reports that younger generations actively negotiate religious and ethnic identities and can influence older generations. This is British Pakistani evidence, not direct Orakzai evidence.
- **Family Language Policy (FLP)**: Khalid, Khan, and Joseph’s 2026 study of a migrant Pashto community in Lahore finds Pashto preferred in home/community domains while Urdu and English serve utility, social mobility, and opportunity functions. This is migrant Pashto evidence in Lahore; it is a comparative model, not direct Orakzai diaspora evidence and not proof of a universal language pattern.

### 4. Individual Leadership and Identity
- **Habib Malik Orakzai (PIHRO)**: Public organizational records document an individual’s professional and human-rights activity. This supports an individual example, not a population or community-wide identity claim.
- **Anum Orakzai**: Search results identify a public profile describing social activism, education, fashion, and community leadership; this should be treated as a public-profile example requiring source verification, not as a general claim about Orakzai women.

## Research Gaps and Limitations
- **Orakzai-Specific Diaspora Statistics**: No official census isolates "Orakzai" as a category in the UK, USA, or Gulf.
- **Long-term Identity Shift**: Limited longitudinal studies on Orakzai-specific identity transformation over four or more generations.

## Identity Evidence Matrix (Analytical Framework)

| **Ethnic (Pashtun)** | Language (Pashto), cultural practices (Attan, food), ANP membership | Broader | High |
