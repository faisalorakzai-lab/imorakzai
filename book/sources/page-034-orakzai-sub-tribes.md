# Sources: Page 34 — Orakzai Sub-Tribes

This document records the academic and historical sources supporting **Page 34: Orakzai Sub-Tribes** in *I’M ORAKZAI*.

## Primary References & Academic Citations

1. **King, Lucas White**: *Monograph on the Orakzai Country and Clans*.
   - **Publication**: Punjab Government Press, 1900.
   - **Details**: The authoritative colonial-era monograph documenting the geography, sections, and clans of the Orakzai tribe.
   - **Claim Supported**: List of major sections (Ismailzai, Daulatzai, Alizai, Muhammad Khel, Sultanzai, Ali Khel, Malla Khel, Mishti, Sheikhan, etc.) and their historical organization.

2. **Encyclopaedia Britannica (1911)**: *Orakzai*.
   - **Publication**: Cambridge University Press.
   - **Details**: Historical entry describing the Tirah region, the Mastura Valley, and the tribal sections of the Orakzai.
   - **Claim Supported**: Geographic distribution in Tirah, seasonal movement patterns, and association of Ali Khel with traditional accounts.

3. **Naval Postgraduate School (NPS)**: *Orakzai Tribe* (Program for Culture and Conflict Studies).
   - **Publication**: NPS Backgrounder / Fact Sheet.
   - **Details**: Modern ethnographic and humanitarian background document identifying the major sections and religious distribution of the Orakzai.
   - **Claim Supported**: Prominence of Ali Khel as one of the largest sections and the identification of Mamozai as a major section in Upper Orakzai.

4. **Combating Terrorism Center (CTC) at West Point**: *Pakistan's Challenges in Orakzai Agency*.
   - **Publication**: *CTC Sentinel*.
   - **Details**: Analysis of tribal dynamics in Orakzai, noting the Ali Khel as the largest tribe in the region.
   - **Claim Supported**: Status of Ali Khel as the largest Orakzai section.

5. **Frontier and Overseas Expeditions from India**: *Volume II: North-West Frontier Tribes*.
   - **Publication**: Intelligence Branch, Army Headquarters India, 1908.
   - **Details**: Military and administrative record providing detailed clan lists and historical contexts for Orakzai sub-divisions.
   - **Claim Supported**: Detailed breakdown of sub-sections and lineages.

## Research Notes on Major Sections

- **Ali Khel**: Consistently identified across historical and modern sources as one of the largest and most influential Orakzai sections.
- **Mamozai**: Identified as a major section, particularly in Upper Orakzai, often associated with the Massuzai or Lashkarzai groups in traditional genealogies but maintaining a distinct and prominent identity.
- **Ham-saya Groups**: Sources like King (1900) distinguish between "original" Orakzai and "ham-saya" (associated) groups like the Mishti and Sheikhan, though these groups are now fully integrated into the Orakzai tribal fabric.
- **Terminology**: The term *Khel* is used fluidly across sources to denote clans, sections, or lineages.
