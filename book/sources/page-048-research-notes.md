# Research Notes: Page 48 — Livestock & Rural Economy

## Role in Livelihood
- **Household Asset**: Livestock represents a "biological bank" for rural families. It can be sold for cash during emergencies or for major expenses (e.g., weddings, education).
- **Income Generation**: Dairy products (milk, butter, ghee) and the sale of animals (especially goats for Eid-ul-Adha) are primary cash sources.
- **Diversification**: Most households combine crop farming with livestock to mitigate yield risks in rain-fed agriculture.
- **Social Capital**: Ownership of certain animals (e.g., cattle, horses historically) can reflect social status, though this is evolving.

## Gender & Labor
- **Women's Contribution**: Women are often the primary caretakers of household livestock, managing feeding, milking, and processing dairy products.
- **Livelihood Empowerment**: Dairy sales often provide women with a degree of financial agency within the household economy.

## Grazing & Land Use
- **Common Lands (Shamilat)**: Grazing traditionally takes place on communal lands, hillsides, and fallow fields.
- **Seasonal Movement**: While not all are nomadic, some seasonal movement of livestock occurs between higher and lower elevations.
- **Rewaj (Custom)**: Grazing rights are often governed by local tribal customs (*Rewaj*).

## Economic Hubs
- **Markets**: Local bazars like Ghiljo and Kalaya serve as trade hubs for livestock and animal products.
- **Trade Routes**: Livestock is often transported to larger markets in Kohat and Peshawar.
