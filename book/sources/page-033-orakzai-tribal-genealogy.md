# Sources: Page 33 — Orakzai Tribal Genealogy

This document records the academic and historical sources supporting **Page 33: Orakzai Tribal Genealogy** in *I’M ORAKZAI*.

## Primary References & Academic Citations

1. **Ni’mat Allāh al-Harawī**: *Makhzan-i Afghānī* (The Treasury of Afghans).
   - **Publication**: 17th-century Mughal court chronicle (English translation by Bernhard Dorn, 1829).
   - **Details**: The foundational text for Pashtun genealogy, describing the three main lineages (Bettani, Sarbani, Gharghusht) and the Karlani branch.
   - **Claim Supported**: Traditional placement of Karlani as an adopted/foundling branch and the progenitor Qais Abdur Rasheed.

2. **Caroe, Olaf**: *The Pathans: 550 B.C.–A.D. 1957*.
   - **Publication**: Oxford University Press / St. Martin's Press.
   - **Details**: Comprehensive history and genealogy of the Pashtun tribes, providing detailed charts of the Karlani confederacy.
   - **Claim Supported**: Karlani split into Kodi and Kaki branches; Orakzai's placement under the Kodi lineage.

3. **Khan, Muhammad Tahir & Khatoon, Dr. Bushra**: *A Comparative Lexical Study of Pashto Dialects (Dawarwala, Orakzai, Bangash)*.
   - **Publication**: *Palatana* (Malakand University Research Journal of Pashto), Vol. 4, No. 1, 2024.
   - **Details**: Discusses the Karlani tribal classification of Orakzai and Bangash in relation to their linguistic characteristics.
   - **Claim Supported**: Modern scholarly use of Karlani classification for Orakzai.

4. **Encyclopaedia Iranica**: *Dawlatzī*, *Ōrakzī* (Search/Related entries).
   - **Publication**: Columbia University / Brill.
   - **Details**: Scholarly entries documenting tribal sections like the Daulatzai and the geographic distribution of Orakzai clans.
   - **Claim Supported**: Identification of major sections (Daulatzai, Muhammad Khel, etc.) and their historical organization.

5. **Frontier and Overseas Expeditions from India**: *Volume II: North-West Frontier Tribes*.
   - **Publication**: Intelligence Branch, Army Headquarters India, 1908.
   - **Details**: Detailed administrative records of tribal sections, sub-sections, and lineages for the Orakzai.
   - **Claim Supported**: List of the seven major sections (Ismailzai, Lashkarzai, Massuzai, Daulatzai, Muhammad Khel, Sturi Khel, Ali Khel) and their respective sub-clans.

## Research Notes & Methodological Distinctions

- **Genealogy vs. History**: The text explicitly distinguishes between "genealogical tradition" and "verified biological history."
- **Kodi vs. Kaki**: While most sources place Orakzai under Kodi, the book notes that tribal classifications can vary between oral tradition and administrative records.
- **Etymology**: The name "Orakzai" is recorded as literally meaning "Lost Son" (*Wrak Zoi*), reflecting a romantic legend rather than a strictly chronological etymology.
