# Research Notes for Page 35: Tribal Clans & Lineages

## Major Orakzai Sections
- **Ali Khel**: Often described as the largest section in Orakzai Agency. Occupies extensive territories in Upper Orakzai. Historically associated with traditional accounts of migration (e.g., from Swat).
- **Mamozai**: A major section in Upper Orakzai. Densely settled in highland valleys. Identified by the author as their ancestral clan.
- **Malla Khel**: Another significant section in Upper Orakzai.
- **Ali Sherzai**: Often mentioned alongside Mamozai and Ali Khel in Upper Orakzai.

## Primary Sources
1. **Lucas White King (1900)**: *Monograph on the Orakzai Country and Clans*.
   - Documents numerous clans and khels.
   - Distinguishes between "original" Orakzai and "ham-saya" (associated) groups.
   - Mentions Ismailzai, Daulatzai, Alizai, Muhammad Khel, Sultanzai, Ali Khel, Malla Khel, Mishti, Sheikhan, etc.
2. **Encyclopaedia Iranica**:
   - Entry for "Dawlatzī" identifies it as one of the seven Orakzai subtribes.
   - References King (1900) for historical data.
3. **Naval Postgraduate School (NPS)**:
   - Identifies six main clans and four hamsaya clans.
   - Notes Ali Khel as one of the hamsaya clans (historically) but highlights its modern prominence.

## Terminology
- **Khel**: Used fluidly for clans, sections, or lineages.
- **Tap**: Used for major tribal sections.
- **Ham-saya**: Historically "associated" or "protected" groups, now integrated into the tribal structure.

## Geographical Context
- **Upper Orakzai**: Home to Ali Khel, Mamozai, Malla Khel, and Ali Sherzai.
- **Lower Orakzai**: Home to other sections like Bizoti, Utman Khel, Feroz Khel, etc.
- **Valleys**: Mastura, Khanki, Kharmana, and Tirah are central to settlement patterns.
