# Sources: Page 99 — Identity in the Diaspora

This source record documents the evidence used for **Page 99: Identity in the Diaspora** in *I’M ORAKZAI*. The page treats identity as personal, contextual, multi-layered, intergenerational, and dynamic. It distinguishes direct Orakzai evidence from Pashtun-wide, Pakistani-diaspora, migrant-Pashto, and general diaspora scholarship.

## Evidence Discipline

Page 99 does not infer Orakzai identity from a surname, birthplace, nationality, religion, language, appearance, or residence. It does not convert Pashtun-wide, Pakistani-wide, Muslim, or general diaspora evidence into Orakzai statistics. It does not fabricate identity surveys, language-retention percentages, marriage rates, generation-specific population estimates, community sizes, or personal stories.

The page distinguishes **direct self-identification**, **family testimony**, **oral history**, **community records**, **academic research**, **public documentation**, and **general diaspora research**. The strength and scope of a claim must match the evidence used to support it. Where direct Orakzai scholarship is scarce, the page labels comparative evidence explicitly and treats research gaps as research gaps.

## Research Gaps

No official global dataset located for this page isolates Orakzai identity across countries, generations, language practice, religion, women’s and youth experiences, marriage patterns, professional identity, digital identity, homeland visits, return migration, mixed families, or intergenerational change. Future research should prioritize consent-based oral history, self-identification, multilingual interviews, destination-specific studies, privacy protection, and transparent uncertainty.
