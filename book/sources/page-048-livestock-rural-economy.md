# Sources: Page 48 — Livestock & Rural Economy

This document records the academic and historical sources supporting **Page 48: Livestock & Rural Economy** in *I’M ORAKZAI*.

## Primary References & Academic Citations

1. **Pakistan Bureau of Statistics (PBS)**: *Livestock Census 2006 (Orakzai Agency)*.
   - **Details**: Provides the historical baseline population for cattle (68,671), buffalo (13,450), sheep (49,951), goats (111,849), camels (259), horses (190), asses (13,390), and mules (2,774).
   - **Claim Supported**: Historical livestock population figures.

2. **Pakistan Bureau of Statistics (PBS) / Scribd**: *District Livestock Population 2024 Census*.
   - **Details**: Recent metadata indicating Orakzai District population: Cattle (64,739), Buffalo (368), Sheep (52,600), Goats (107,556), Camels (44), Horses (21), Mules (2,971), and Asses (4,212).
   - **Claim Supported**: Recent population trends and shifts in animal categories.

3. **FAO Pakistan / UN FAO**: *Livestock-based Livelihood Assistance in Merged Areas*.
   - **Details**: Highlights the role of livestock as a primary source of income and survival for farming families in Orakzai.
   - **Claim Supported**: Economic role of livestock; income generation from dairy products.

4. **UN Women Pakistan**: *Status of Women and Girls in Orakzai District (2020)*.
   - **Details**: Documents the critical role of women in the agricultural and livestock economy, particularly in care and dairy processing.
   - **Claim Supported**: Gender roles in livestock management; women's contribution to household economy.

5. **Khyber Pakhtunkhwa Livestock & Dairy Development Department**: *District Profiles and Vaccination Drives*.
   - **Details**: Records the provision of veterinary services and the importance of livestock in the regional rural development strategy.
   - **Claim Supported**: Government support systems; significance of animal health for livelihoods.

6. **Khyber Pakhtunkhwa Tribal Decade Strategy**: *Merged Areas Development Plan*.
   - **Details**: Discusses grazing practices on common lands (*Shamilat*) and the integration of livestock with crop residues.
   - **Claim Supported**: Grazing systems; crop-livestock interaction.

## Verified Economic Data

- **The "Biological Bank"**: Livestock serves as a mobile asset for wealth storage and risk mitigation.
- **Goat Dominance**: Goats remain the most numerous livestock category, reflecting their suitability for mountainous terrain.
- **Transport Shift**: The decrease in asses and horses suggests a modernization of rural transport and agricultural machinery.
- **Dairy Economy**: Milk and ghee production are fundamental to both household consumption and cash sales.
- **Market Integration**: Local commercial hubs like Ghiljo and Kalaya facilitate the livestock trade.
