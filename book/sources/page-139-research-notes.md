# Research Notes: Page 139 — Blockchain Infrastructure

This document records the research findings and methodological decisions for **Page 139: Blockchain Infrastructure**.

## 1. Global Market & Technology Trends (2026)
- **Market Valuation:** The global blockchain market is estimated at **$54.08 billion in 2026**, reflecting accelerated digital transformation across industries (MarketsandMarkets).
- **Modular Revolution:** 2026 marks the mainstream adoption of **Modular Blockchain Architecture**, which separates core functions: Execution, Settlement, Consensus, and Data Availability (DA).
- **Data Availability (DA):** Specialized DA layers are driving a scalability revolution, allowing specialized chains to serve specific use cases like gaming and finance while inheriting security from base layers.

## 2. Technical Infrastructure Stack
- **The Stack:** A multi-layered system comprising Hardware/Cloud, Storage, Networking, Consensus, Execution, Smart Contracts, and Applications.
- **Node Ecosystem:** Diverse node roles include Full Nodes (independent verification), Validator Nodes (consensus participation), Archival Nodes (historical state), and Light Clients (resource-efficient interaction).
- **Networking & RPC:** RPC infrastructure serves as the critical bridge between users/applications and the peer-to-peer blockchain network. Indexers and explorers provide the necessary transparency and performance layers for production applications.

## 3. Pakistan's Infrastructure Landscape (2026)
- **Power Allocation:** The Government of Pakistan has allocated **2,000 megawatts (MW)** of state grid electricity to fuel Bitcoin mining and AI data centers, marking a major stride in digital infrastructure (MoITT/Finance Ministry).
- **Tier III Data Centers:** Initiatives like the **Quantum Global Data Centre (QGDC)** are building Pakistan's largest Tier III data centers with significant foreign investment ($230M), providing the hardware foundation for national blockchain and AI projects.
- **Sovereign Capacity:** National AI data centers and Centres of Excellence are being established to train 200,000 individuals annually, building the human and technical capital required for sovereign digital infrastructure.
