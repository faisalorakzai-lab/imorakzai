# Research Notes: Page 61 — Orakzai Music

## Core Concept
- **Music as Cultural Memory**: Music in Orakzai is not merely entertainment but a vehicle for preserving oral history, landscape references, and community emotions.
- **Evidence-Based Documentation**: Prioritizing direct Orakzai-specific evidence from academic studies while situating it within the broader Pashtun musical world.

## Key Findings

### 1. The Orakzai Folk Songs Study (2025)
- **Source**: Dr. Shakir “Orakzai”, *The Orakzai Folk Songs*, PASTO Quarterly, International Center for Pashto Studies, Academy of Sciences of Afghanistan, Spring/Summer 2025.
- **Key Documented Forms**:
    - **Tappa**: A fundamental Pashto poetic/song form, described as one of the oldest in the region.
    - **Tekai**: An Orakzai-specific structural element where Tappas (folk verses) are inserted between main song sections.
    - **Sail**: A documented festive gathering of village women on Eid nights, often near springs or trees, featuring singing and recitation.
- **Thematic Content**:
    - **Landscape**: References to Tirah, Daburi, Mastura spring, snow, and rain.
    - **Agriculture**: Documented songs about the wheat harvest, linking work to melody.
    - **Migration**: A song referencing Delhi, interpreted as memory of historical Pashtun travel for livelihoods.
    - **Weddings**: Songs documenting family separation and bridal transitions.

### 2. Pashtun-Wide Musical Framework
- **Major Forms (AIPS)**: Tappa, Charbeta, Neemkai, Loba, Shaan, Badala.
- **Instruments**: Rubab (regional string instrument), Mangay (percussion), Zerbaghali, Tabla, and Harmonium (modern).
- **Attan**: A national Pashtun dance with broad regional distribution, not uniquely Orakzai.

### 3. Contemporary Orakzai Musical Events
- **Orakzai Spring Mela (2024)**: A three-day festival in Kharashakhwa Storikhel featuring music concerts alongside traditional sports (wrestling, stone lifting) and modern activities (paragliding, jeep races).
- **Digital Transmission**: Use of social media and digital archives for preserving folk melodies and sharing contemporary Pashto music.

### 4. Cultural & Social Context
- **Oral Transmission**: Folklore and songs transmitted intergenerationally through memory and community performance.
- **Religious Intersection**: Distinguishing between cultural musical practices and Islamic theological interpretations; role of devotional forms like Naat and Hamd.
- **Women’s Role**: Documented gatherings (Sail) as spaces for emotional and social expression through song.

## Evidence Hierarchy
- **A. Orakzai-Specific**: Dr. Shakir Orakzai’s 2025 study on folk songs, Tekai, and Sail; 2024 Spring Mela music concerts.
- **B. Pashtun-Wide**: Tappa and other poetic forms; Rubab traditions; Attan dance.
- **C. Regional**: KP musical landscape; regional instruments; contemporary Pashto music production.
- **D. Islamic**: Devotional poetry (Naat/Hamd); Eid celebrations.
- **E. Contemporary**: Digital archiving; modern festival programming; urban music adaptation.

## Documented Terminology
- **Tappa**: Oldest Pashto folk verse form.
- **Tekai**: Orakzai-specific song insertions.
- **Sail**: Women’s festive singing gathering.
- **Rubab**: Traditional string instrument.
- **Mangay**: Clay pot percussion.
- **Melmastia**: Hospitality (often accompanied by music in Hujras).
