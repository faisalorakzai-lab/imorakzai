# Sources: Page 17 — Pashtunwali — The Code of Life

This document records the academic and historical sources supporting **Page 17: Pashtunwali — The Code of Life** in *I’M ORAKZAI*.

## Primary References & Academic Citations

1. **Encyclopaedia Iranica & Anthropological Scholarship**: *Pashtun Customary Framework and Social Organization*.
   - **Authors**: Cultural anthropologists and historians specializing in Afghan and South Asian societies.
   - **Publisher**: Encyclopædia Iranica Foundation, New York.
   - **Details**: Analyzes Pashtunwali as a living customary normative system rather than a fixed written code.
   - **Claim Supported**: Definition of Pashtunwali as a customary social framework.

2. **Akbar S. Ahmed & eHRAF World Cultures (Yale University)**: *Tenets of Pashtunwali (Nang, Melmastia, Nanawatai, Badal)*.
   - **Authors**: Akbar S. Ahmed and comparative ethnographers.
   - **Details**: Comprehensive documentation of core ethical principles, honour (nang), hospitality (melmastia), asylum (nanawatai), and reciprocity (badal) in tribal societies.
   - **Claim Supported**: Core normative pillars of Pashtunwali.

3. **Encyclopaedia Iranica**: *The Jirga Assembly and Customary Mediation*.
   - **Details**: Documents the structure, consensus-based decision-making, and dispute-resolution mechanisms of the traditional Pashtun jirga.
   - **Claim Supported**: Institutional role of the jirga and community deliberation.

4. **Academic Studies on Custom and Religion**: *Interaction Between Pashtunwali and Islamic Norms*.
   - **Details**: Examines the historical coexistence and dialogue between customary tribal practice and Islamic jurisprudence in Pashtun communities.
   - **Claim Supported**: Relationship between custom, religious norms, and modern social evolution.
