# Sources: Page 67 — The Role of Women

This document records the academic and ethnographic sources supporting **Page 67: The Role of Women** in *I’M ORAKZAI*.

## Primary References & Academic Citations

1. **Naseer, N., Mahsud, M. I., & Fatima, M.** (2026). "Early Warning by Women for Conflict Prevention in the Erstwhile Federally Administered Tribal Areas (Merged Districts)". *Conflict Studies Quarterly*.
   - **Details**: Qualitative study documenting how Orakzai and Kurram women identify conflict triggers and advise elders.
   - **Claim Supported**: Women's agency in conflict prevention and community safety.

2. **Orakzai, M. A.** (2025). "Globalization's Influence on Pashtun Cultural Values: A Study of Proverbs about Women across Different Age Groups". *Pakistan Journal of Society, Education and Language*.
   - **Details**: Research on how proverbs define and transmit social expectations for women in Orakzai.
   - **Claim Supported**: The role of proverbs in cultural identity and intergenerational change.

3. **Sheikh, M. R.** (2026). "Nexus between sociocultural practices and maternal mortality in Pakistan: a geospatial analysis". *BMC Public Health*.
   - **Details**: Analysis of how cultural norms in districts like Orakzai affect women's health access.
   - **Claim Supported**: Impact of social practices on maternal health outcomes.

4. **Gallup Pakistan / Pakistan Bureau of Statistics**. (2023). "Literacy Between the 2017 and 2023 Censuses".
   - **Details**: Official data showing Orakzai's female literacy remains below 30%.
   - **Claim Supported**: Education statistics and the gender literacy gap.

5. **Safdar, M.** (2021). "Women Farmers' Access to and Control Over Farming Resources in rural areas of Khyber Pakhtunkhwa". *ResearchersLinks*.
   - **Details**: Study on the essential but often invisible role of women in the agricultural economy.
   - **Claim Supported**: Women's contribution to agriculture and livestock management.
