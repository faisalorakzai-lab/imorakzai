# Research Notes: Page 106 — Digitizing Tribal Archives

This document records the research findings and methodological decisions for **Page 106: Digitizing Tribal Archives**.

## 1. Defining "Tribal Archives"
- **Conceptual Shift:** Moving away from a monolithic view of archives. Tribal archives are diverse, held by families, elders, religious institutions, and community organizations.
- **Components:** Family collections (genealogies, letters), oral histories, manuscripts (Sufi shrines, religious texts), and official colonial/state records.
- **Pashtun Context:** Focus on the "Endangered Archives" project (UCLA MEAP) which highlights manuscripts from Sufi shrines in Pashtun tribal regions as a model for community-held records.

## 2. Archival Ethics & Community Sovereignty
- **Metadata as Radical Care:** Research emphasizes that sustainable metadata practices must center community sovereignty and harm reduction.
- **Consent & Privacy:** Archiving oral history requires strict adherence to narrator intentions and ongoing integrity.
- **Digital Equity:** Ensuring that digitization does not merely extract knowledge but serves the community.

## 3. Archival Landscape in Pakistan
- **National Archives of Pakistan:** Holds private collections of leaders and newspapers, but often lacks granular tribal community records.
- **Punjab Archives, Lahore:** Contains Mughal, pre-colonial, and colonial records relevant to the broader region.
- **Khyber Pakhtunkhwa (KP) Archives:** The most direct official source for frontier and tribal administration records.
- **Endangered Archives Programme (British Library):** A key resource for identifying and digitizing vulnerable records in the region.

## 4. Digitization Workflows & Standards
- **UNESCO Memory of the World:** Safeguarding documentary heritage through preservation and protected access.
- **3-2-1 Strategy:** A resilience principle (3 copies, 2 media, 1 offsite) but not a substitute for a full preservation plan.
- **Provenance:** Critical to document the chain of custody (Creator → Owner → Custodian → Digitizer).

## 5. AI and the Digital Archive
- **Applications:** OCR for Perso-Arabic scripts, transcription assistance, and metadata tagging.
- **Risks:** Hallucinations, misidentification, and generative "restoration" that fabricates history.
- **Verification:** Human review is the final authority; AI is an assistant.

## 6. Evidence Boundaries & Cautions
- **No Claims of Centralization:** Orakzai records are scattered; no single "Orakzai Archive" exists.
- **Authenticity:** Digitization does not automatically prove a source's historical status.
- **Privacy:** Heightened care for living individuals and sensitive family data.
