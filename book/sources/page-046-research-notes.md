# Research Notes: Page 46 — Traditional Settlements

## Definition & Context
- **Traditional Settlement**: Patterns of habitation and built environment developed in response to local geography, climate, and social needs before the dominance of modern urban infrastructure.
- **Geographic Determinism**: In Orakzai, settlement is a "system" where terrain dictates location. Access to water, cultivable land, and transit routes are primary factors.

## Settlement Patterns
1. **Clustered Settlements**: Common in mountainous areas. Houses are built in close proximity or shared compounds.
   - **Drivers**: Kinship (Khel), shared resources, security, and limited flat land.
2. **Density**: Varies from low-density dispersed households on slopes to dense clusters in valley basins (e.g., Mastura and Khanki valleys).
3. **Terracing**: While not universal, terraced fields and occasionally houses are documented on steeper slopes to maximize usable space.

## Architecture & Materials
- **Vernacular Materials**:
  - **Walls**: Stone masonry and mud are dominant (approx. 49% stone/mud in Tirah area; 45% brick masonry in modern contexts).
  - **Ceilings/Doors**: Timber (locally sourced wood) used for structural support and apertures.
- **The Compound (Qila/Kala)**:
  - **Structure**: A walled enclosure organizing household life.
  - **Components**: Living rooms, storage for grain/fodder, livestock shelters, and a central courtyard.
  - **Functions**: Privacy (Purda), security, and protection from extreme weather (transitional climate).

## Communal Spaces
1. **Hujra**:
   - **Role**: Secular community center, guest house, and meeting place for men.
   - **Placement**: Often at the entrance of a settlement or centrally located for accessibility.
2. **Mosque**:
   - **Role**: Religious hub and community center.
   - **Materials**: Traditionally stone and wood (e.g., Jamia Masjid in Tirah often featuring significant woodwork).

## Social-Spatial Relationship
- **Kinship-Neighborhood Nexus**: Settlements are often organized by *Khel* (clan) or extended family units.
- **Access**: Traditional settlements followed natural routes and mountain passes (Kandao).
