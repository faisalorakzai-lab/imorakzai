# Sources: Page 96 — Orakzai Across Pakistan

This document records the primary and academic sources supporting **Page 96: Orakzai Across Pakistan** in *I’M ORAKZAI*.

## Primary Academic & Research Sources

1. **UNHCR / Protection Cluster.** (2016). *Return Intention Survey - Orakzai Agency*.
   - **Details**: A comprehensive survey of displaced families from Orakzai Agency regarding their intentions and challenges to return.
   - **Claim Supported**: Specific documentation of Orakzai displacement, return intentions, and challenges (e.g., destroyed housing).

2. **United Nations Population Fund (UNFPA).** (2013/2022). *Internal Migration and Urbanization in Pakistan*.
   - **Details**: Reports on the drivers and impacts of internal migration on Pakistan's urban growth and labour markets.
   - **Claim Supported**: Pakistan-wide evidence for internal migration drivers (employment, education, services).

3. **Mahmood, S., Atiq, A., Samiullah, & Khan, A. R.** (2023). *Socio-Economic Effects of War Against Terror-Induced Displacement on Host Communities in District Kohat, Pakistan*.
   - **Details**: Analyzes the impact of Orakzai TDPs (Temporarily Displaced Persons) on neighboring Kohat and their return processes.
   - **Claim Supported**: Regional mobility and displacement links between Orakzai and Kohat.

4. **Government of Khyber Pakhtunkhwa.** (2024). *Official District Profile: Orakzai*.
   - **Details**: Documents the administrative boundaries of Orakzai District (Khyber, Kohat, Hangu, Kurram).
   - **Claim Supported**: Geographic context and regional neighbors.

## Research Gaps & Limitations
- **Sub-Tribe Census Data**: Official census data does not provide a sub-tribe level breakdown for Orakzai residents living in Sindh, Punjab, or Balochistan.
- **National Diaspora Number**: There is no verified national total for the Orakzai population outside the district; figures are limited to specific regional or urban studies.
- **Plausible Pathways**: While mobility pathways to major cities are documented, the exact volume of movement remains an area for future research.
