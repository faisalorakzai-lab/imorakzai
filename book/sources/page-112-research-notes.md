# Research Notes: Page 112 — The Rise of the Internet

This document records the research findings and methodological decisions for **Page 112: The Rise of the Internet**.

## 1. Global Internet History
- **ARPANET (1969):** The first operational packet-switching network. The first message was sent between UCLA and SRI International on October 29, 1969. The intended word was "LOGIN," but the system crashed after "LO."
- **TCP/IP (1983):** Developed by Vint Cerf and Bob Kahn. The official transition of ARPANET to TCP/IP occurred on January 1, 1983, marking the technical birth of the modern internet.
- **World Wide Web (1989):** Proposed by Tim Berners-Lee at CERN as an information management system. It introduced HTML, HTTP, and URLs. The first website went live in 1991.
- **Commercialization (1990s):** The lifting of restrictions on commercial traffic and the emergence of graphical browsers (Mosaic, Netscape) led to mass adoption.

## 2. Internet in Pakistan
- **Early Phase (1992–1994):** Imran-Net introduced dial-up email services (1992-93). Digicom launched the first full dial-up internet service in 1994.
- **Growth (2000s):** Deregulation and the establishment of the Pakistan Telecommunication Authority (PTA) led to a surge in ISPs and broadband adoption.
- **Mobile Revolution (2014–Present):** The launch of 3G and 4G services transformed internet access from a luxury to a necessity, with over 150 million users by 2025.

## 3. Orakzai and the Digital Community
- **Connectivity:** Orakzai's digital landscape was significantly transformed following the tribal areas' merger with Khyber Pakhtunkhwa (2018). Mobile broadband is the primary mode of access.
- **Impact:** The internet has enabled remote education, global diaspora connections, and the digital preservation of Pashto culture and Orakzai history.
- **Digital Divide:** Infrastructure challenges, including electricity and network stability in mountainous terrain, remain barriers to equitable access.

## 4. Technical Distinctions
- **Internet:** The underlying global network of networks (hardware and protocols).
- **Web:** A service running on the internet (documents and links).
- **Packet Switching:** The fundamental technology of breaking data into small chunks for efficient routing.

## 5. Evidence Boundaries
- **Primary Sources:** ICANN, IETF, CERN, PTA, and SBP.
- **Terminology:** Strict distinction between the Internet and the World Wide Web.
- **Dates:** Milestones are dated based on authoritative institutional records (e.g., CERN for the Web, DARPA for ARPANET).
