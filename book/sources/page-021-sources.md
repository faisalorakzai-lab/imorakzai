# Sources: Page 21 — Nang — Honour

This document records the academic and historical sources supporting **Page 21: Nang — Honour** in *I’M ORAKZAI*.

## Primary References & Academic Citations

1. **M. Jamil Hanifi (Encyclopaedia Iranica)**: *JERGA (Pashtunwali and Nang)*.
   - **Author**: M. Jamil Hanifi.
   - **Publication**: *Encyclopaedia Iranica*, Vol. XIV, Fasc. 6, pp. 630–632, 2008 (updated 2012).
   - **Publisher**: Encyclopædia Iranica Foundation, New York.
   - **Details**: Identifies Pashtunwali as a charter of appropriate behavior, highlighting male honor as *nang* or *ʿezzat*, alongside avoiding shame (*sharm*), melmastia, nanawatai, and badal.
   - **Claim Supported**: Definition of nang within the Pashtunwali code and its relationship with social conduct.

2. **Nancy Hatch Dupree (Encyclopaedia Iranica)**: *ETIQUETTE iii. Etiquette in Afghanistan*.
   - **Author**: Nancy Hatch Dupree.
   - **Publication**: *Encyclopaedia Iranica*, Vol. IX, Fasc. 1, pp. 44–47.
   - **Publisher**: Encyclopædia Iranica Foundation, New York.
   - **Details**: Documents conventional rules of polite personal behavior (*ādāb-e moʿāšarat*), social reputation, respect, and social esteem (*ʿezzat*).
   - **Claim Supported**: Social etiquette, respect, and reputation in Pashtun society.

3. **Lutz Rzehak (Afghanistan Analysts Network)**: *Doing Pashto: Pashtunwali as the Ideal of Honourable Behaviour and Tribal Life among the Pashtuns*.
   - **Author**: Lutz Rzehak.
   - **Publication**: Afghanistan Analysts Network, 2011.
   - **Details**: Examines how "Doing Pashto" equates to acting honorably and being guided by the ethical norms of Pashtunwali.
   - **Claim Supported**: Honor as active conduct and behavioral ideal.

4. **Mikhail Pelevin (Cambridge University Press)**: *Seeking Protection and Reconciliation: A Pashtun Legal Custom in Recorded Tribal Histories*.
   - **Author**: Mikhail Pelevin (St. Petersburg State University).
   - **Publication**: *International Journal of Middle East Studies*, 54(4), 2022, pp. 687–704.
   - **Publisher**: Cambridge University Press.
   - **Details**: Analyzes customary norms, distinguishing broader honour (*nang*) from specific conflict-response mechanisms (*badal*).
   - **Claim Supported**: Distinction between nang (general honour/conduct) and badal (retribution).
