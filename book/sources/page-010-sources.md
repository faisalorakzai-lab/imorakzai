# Sources: Page 10 — Who Are the Pashtuns?

This document records the academic and historical sources supporting **Page 10: Who Are the Pashtuns?** in *I’M ORAKZAI*.

## Primary References & Academic Citations

1. **Encyclopaedia Iranica & Encyclopædia Britannica**: *Pashtun Ethnolinguistic Identity*.
   - **Authors**: Academic specialists in Iranian and Central Asian studies.
   - **Publisher**: Encyclopædia Iranica Foundation & Encyclopædia Britannica.
   - **Details**: Defines Pashtuns as a major ethnolinguistic community inhabiting Afghanistan and northwestern Pakistan, united by language, culture, and tribal networks.
   - **Claim Supported**: Ethnolinguistic definition and geographic distribution.

2. **Encyclopaedia Iranica**: *Pashto Language & Eastern Iranian Classification*.
   - **Authors**: Linguists specializing in Iranian philology.
   - **Details**: Establishes Pashto as an Eastern Iranian language within the Indo-European family, detailing regional dialects and contact phenomena.
   - **Claim Supported**: Pashto linguistic lineage and dialectal diversity.

3. **Academic Anthropology & Ethnography**: *Tribal Organization and Social Structure*.
   - **Authors**: Anthropologists studying tribal societies in South Asia.
   - **Details**: Examines the role of clans, tribal confederations, and genealogical traditions in Pashtun society without reducing them to rigid biological categories.
   - **Claim Supported**: Complexity of Pashtun tribal organization and oral genealogies.

4. **Historical & Scholarly Literature**: *The Question of Ancient Origins*.
   - **Details**: Analyzes historical theories concerning Pashtun ethnogenesis, distinguishing between traditional origin accounts (such as legendary ancestors) and empirical historical-linguistic evidence.
   - **Claim Supported**: Scholarly caution regarding single ancient-origin theories.

5. **Modern Migration & Urban Studies**: *Global Pashtun Diaspora and Contemporary Life*.
   - **Details**: Documents the demographic shift of Pashtuns into major urban centers (Peshawar, Karachi, Kabul, Dubai, London) and professional spheres.
   - **Claim Supported**: The urban and global expansion of modern Pashtun identity.
