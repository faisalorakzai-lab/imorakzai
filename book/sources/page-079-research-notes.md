# Research Notes: Page 79 — Colonial Records & Historical Sources

## Core Concept
- **Source Literacy**: Orakzai history is reconstructed from a mosaic of sources—colonial, governmental, academic, and oral. Each source requires critical analysis to understand its purpose, bias, and limitations.
- **Archival Traces**: Colonial records (Gazetteers, Tribal Tables, Administration Reports) provide detailed administrative and military data but reflect an "imperial gaze."
- **Relational Evidence**: Cross-checking colonial documentation with local oral history and modern academic research creates a more complete and balanced historical narrative.

## Key Findings

### 1. Major Colonial Collections
- **India Office Records (IOR)**: The primary repository for British Indian administrative files. Key series include:
    - **L/P&S (Political and Secret Department)**: Contains "Secret Border Reports" and correspondence regarding tribal affairs and the Durand Line.
    - **V/10 (Administration Reports)**: NWFP Administration Reports (1901–1947) documenting yearly political and economic changes.
- **Gazetteers**: The *Gazetteer of the Kohat District (1883-84)* is a foundational primary source, detailing Orakzai clans, geography, and relations with the British.
- **Tribal Tables & Dictionaries**: *A Dictionary of the Pathan Tribes on the North-West Frontier of India (1910)* serves as a classification manual for the General Staff, indexing tribes and subdivisions.
- **Source**: *India Office Records* (British Library); *Dictionary of Pathan Tribes* (1910).

### 2. Military & Expedition Records
- **Expedition Reports**: Detailed accounts of military actions (e.g., the 1897 Tirah Expedition). These records provide precise dates, routes, and lists of "belligerent" clans (Afridi, Orakzai).
- **Parliamentary Papers**: Collections like the "Orakzai and Black Mountain Expeditions" files provide political justifications for military interventions and official returns of casualties and fines.
- **Source**: *The Tirah Campaign* (Mills, 1898); *Black Mountain Expeditions* (Archive.org).

### 3. Cartographic Sources
- **Frontier Maps**: Colonial survey maps (e.g., Survey of India) recorded physical geography, routes, and administrative boundaries. However, these often projected state frameworks onto tribal territories.
- **Source**: *India Office Map Collection* (British Library).

### 4. Local & Oral Sources
- **Oral Memory**: Family stories and elder testimonies preserve details missing from official archives—everyday life, women's experiences, and internal tribal dynamics.
- **Pashto Poetry/Proverbs**: Serve as cultural archives, reflecting moral codes (Pashtunwali) and collective memory of historical events.
- **Source**: *Khudai Khidmatgar Resistance* (Gul Orakzai/ProQuest); *Pashtunwali* (Academic Ethnography).

### 5. Source Criticism Methodology
- **The Imperial Gaze**: Recognizing that colonial records were created for administration and control, not for community self-representation.
- **Archival Silence**: Identifying missing voices—women, non-combatants, and dissenting local voices—in official documentation.
- **Corroboration**: The principle of cross-referencing multiple independent sources (e.g., a British military report vs. an Orakzai oral tradition) to establish historical confidence.

## Evidence Hierarchy
- **Level 1: Direct Evidence**: Verified archival documents, contemporary maps, official reports.
- **Level 2: Corroborated Evidence**: Claims supported by both colonial records and oral history.
- **Level 3: Reasonable Inference**: Interpretations based on existing data where direct evidence is missing.
- **Level 4: Research Gaps**: Explicitly identified areas where evidence is insufficient (e.g., women's everyday history in the 19th century).

## Confidence Labels
- **High**: Multiple independent primary sources.
- **Medium**: Single primary source or corroborated secondary source.
- **Low**: Oral tradition without external corroboration.
- **Insufficient**: No reliable record found.
