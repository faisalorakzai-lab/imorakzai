# Research Notes: Page 56 — Orakzai Weddings

## Core Concept
- **Wedding as a Nexus**: A meeting point of family, kinship, religion, custom, and community.
- **Identity & Change**: Traditions are not static; they adapt to urban life, migration, and modern technology.

## Key Findings

### 1. Islamic Foundation & Legal Framework
- **Nikah**: The essential Islamic marriage contract involving consent, offer/acceptance, and Mahr.
- **Mahr (Haq Mehr)**: A mandatory payment from groom to bride, distinct from customary "bride price" or dowry (Jahez).
- **Consent**: A core principle in both Islamic law and modern human rights, often contrasted with problematic customary practices like Swara (forced marriage as dispute resolution).

### 2. Family & Kinship
- **Matchmaking**: Traditionally handled through family networks and elders.
- **Consanguinity**: Cousin marriage is documented in Pashtun societies as a cultural preference for maintaining family land and kinship bonds, though not a religious requirement.
- **Negotiation**: Involves parents, elders, and extended family members.

### 3. Wedding Ceremonies & Rituals
- **Pre-wedding**: Family gatherings, community visits, and preparations.
- **Mehndi (Henna)**: A regional South Asian/Pakistani tradition widely adopted in Pashtun weddings.
- **Nikah Ceremony**: The formal religious and legal union.
- **Walima**: The wedding feast hosted by the groom's family, emphasized in Islamic tradition as a communal celebration.

### 4. Visual & Material Culture
- **Clothing**: Groom often wears traditional Shalwar Kameez with a waistcoat and sometimes a Turban (Lungee). Bride wears embroidered garments (Firaq Partug) and jewelry.
- **Food**: Communal feasts involving staples like rice (Polao), meat (Gwashta), and bread (Doday). Sweets (Mishti) are essential for celebration.
- **Music & Dance**: Rabab music, big drums, and Attan (communal dance) are documented in Orakzai and broader Pashtun contexts.

### 5. Social Spaces
- **Hujra**: Serves as the primary space for male guests and community gatherings during weddings.
- **Women's Space**: Private domestic spaces where women perform rituals, songs, and celebrations.

### 6. Modern Adaptation
- **Urbanization**: Shift to marriage halls, hotels, and professional catering in cities like Peshawar or Islamabad.
- **Digital Culture**: WhatsApp invitations, video calls for diaspora family members, and online photography archives.
- **Migration**: Diaspora weddings blend traditional Orakzai/Pashtun elements with the customs of their new countries.

## Evidence Hierarchy
- **A. Orakzai-Specific**: Village-level negotiation records; historical photographs of Tirah weddings; local music/Rabab traditions (Jay Kumar Orakzai).
- **B. Pashtun-Wide**: Nikah/Mahr practices; Attan dance; Hujra role; Pashtunwali links to honour/dignity.
- **C. Regional**: Mehndi rituals; regional food staples; former FATA marriage demographic studies.
- **D. Islamic**: Legal requirements for Nikah, consent, and Mahr.
- **E. Pakistani Modern**: Marriage halls; professional photography; digital invitations.
- **F. Oral History**: Grandparents' accounts of traditional proposals and village ceremonies.
