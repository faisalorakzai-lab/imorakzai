# Sources: Page 61 — Orakzai Music

This document records the academic and historical sources supporting **Page 61: Orakzai Music** in *I’M ORAKZAI*.

## Primary References & Academic Citations

1. **Orakzai, S. (Dr.)**: *The Orakzai Folk Songs*. PASTO Quarterly, International Center for Pashto Studies, Academy of Sciences of Afghanistan. Round 2, Vol. 61–62, Serial No. 112–113, Spring/Summer 2025, pp. 106–118.
   - **Details**: A direct ethnographic and literary study documenting folk songs, oral transmission, and specific cultural practices among Orakzai communities.
   - **Claim Supported**: Orakzai folk songs, Tekai structure, Sail gatherings, Eid-night singing, harvest songs, and landscape references (Tirah, Daburi).

2. **American Institute of Pakistan Studies (AIPS)**: *Pashto Folk Music and Poetic Forms*.
   - **Details**: Documentation of Tappa, Charbeta, Neemkai, Loba, Shaan, and Badala within the broader Pashtun musical tradition.
   - **Claim Supported**: Pashtun-wide musical forms and the role of the Rubab.

3. **Radio Pakistan / The Express Tribune**: *Reports on Orakzai Spring Mela 2024*. (June 2024).
   - **Details**: Media coverage of the three-day festival in Kharashakhwa Storikhel, documenting music concerts and traditional sports.
   - **Claim Supported**: Contemporary public musical events in Orakzai District.

4. **Pakistan Bureau of Statistics (PBS)**: *7th Population and Housing Census (2023) - KP District Report*.
   - **Details**: Data on language and dialect distribution in Orakzai and surrounding districts.
   - **Claim Supported**: Pashto/Pakhto as the primary language of cultural transmission.

5. **Ahmed, A. S.**: *Pukhtun Economy and Society*. (1980).
   - **Details**: Ethnographic analysis of tribal social life, including communal gatherings and hospitality.
   - **Claim Supported**: Social context of musical performance in tribal settings.
