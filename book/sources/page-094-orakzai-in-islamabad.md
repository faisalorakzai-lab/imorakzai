# Sources: Page 94 — Orakzai in Islamabad

This document records the primary and academic sources supporting **Page 94: Orakzai in Islamabad** in *I’M ORAKZAI*.

## Primary Academic & Research Sources

1. **Shah, A., et al.** (2021). *Four decadal urban land degradation in Pakistan: a case study of capital city Islamabad during 1979–2019*. *Scientific Reports*.
   - **Details**: Documents the expansion of built-up area in Islamabad by 41.7% over four decades.
   - **Claim Supported**: Islamabad's urban expansion and land-use change data.

2. **Khan, J. A., et al.** (2023). *Exploring the nexus between land use land cover (LULC) changes and urbanization*. *Sustainability*.
   - **Details**: Reports that built-up area in Islamabad increased from 5.7% (52 km²) in 1990 to 25.7% (233 km²) in 2021.
   - **Claim Supported**: Specific urbanization and built-up area growth figures.

3. **Quaid-i-Azam University (QAU).** (2024). *Partnership in Human Rights Research and Student Engagement*.
   - **Details**: Records engagement with Malik Habib Orakzai, Chairman of PIHRO, regarding research and student activities.
   - **Claim Supported**: Orakzai professional and institutional presence in Islamabad.

4. **Bukhari, A. Q.** (2026). *Layered Continuity in Pakistan's Northwest Borderlands: State, Power, and Political Economy*.
   - **Details**: Analyzes the synthesis of tribal identity and statehood in the urban professional context.
   - **Claim Supported**: Conceptual framework for identity synthesis in the capital.

## Research Gaps & Limitations
- **Direct Demographic Evidence**: There is currently no publicly available census data that isolates the Orakzai population within the Islamabad Capital Territory.
- **Neighborhood Mapping**: Orakzai families in Islamabad are dispersed across various sectors; there are no documented "Orakzai-majority" neighborhoods in the capital.
- **Migration Volume**: While professional and student presence is verified, the total volume of Orakzai migration to Islamabad remains a research gap compared to the larger hubs of Karachi or Peshawar.
