# Sources: Page 57 — Birth & Family Traditions

This document records the academic and historical sources supporting **Page 57: Birth & Family Traditions** in *I’M ORAKZAI*.

## Primary References & Academic Citations

1. **Ahmed, A. S.**: *Pukhtun Economy and Society: Traditional Structure and Economic Development in a Tribal Society*. (1980).
   - **Details**: Classic ethnography discussing the social significance of birth in tribal structure and the role of family in early socialization.
   - **Claim Supported**: Tribal and family involvement in early childhood; social role of birth.

2. **Oxford Dictionary of Islam**: *Aqiqah*.
   - **Details**: Defines the Islamic birth ritual involving shaving of the child's head, animal sacrifice, and naming.
   - **Claim Supported**: Islamic foundation of Aqiqah and naming rituals.

3. **Naseer, N.**: *Pakistan: Early Warning by Women for Conflict Prevention in the Erstwhile FATA*. (2026).
   - **Details**: Fieldwork conducted in Kurram and Orakzai districts discussing the impact of conflict on family life and the absence of accurate birth records.
   - **Claim Supported**: Orakzai-specific context; challenges in birth documentation.

4. **Ferro-Luzzi, G. E.**: *Ritual as Language: The Case of South Indian Food Offerings*. (1977).
   - **Details**: Anthropological study on the meaning of food in rituals, applicable to regional hospitality practices.
   - **Claim Supported**: Role of food and hospitality in birth celebrations.

5. **Historical Records of the North-West Frontier**: *Ethnographic accounts of tribal life*.
   - **Details**: British-era records describing the ceremonies and communal celebrations of birth in the frontier regions.
   - **Claim Supported**: Historical birth ceremonies; role of family and community.
