# Sources: Page 11 — Origins of the Pashtun People

This document records the academic and historical sources supporting **Page 11: Origins of the Pashtun People** in *I’M ORAKZAI*.

## Primary References & Academic Citations

1. **Encyclopaedia Iranica**: *Paṣ̌tō language* & *Eastern Iranian Languages*.
   - **Authors**: Iranian philologists and linguists.
   - **Publisher**: Encyclopædia Iranica Foundation, New York.
   - **Details**: Establishes Pashto as an Eastern Iranian language within the Indo-European family, detailing its phonology, historical development, and regional relations.
   - **Claim Supported**: Linguistic evidence and classification of Pashto.

2. **Encyclopaedia Iranica & Oxford Academic**: *Early Historical References and Ethnonyms*.
   - **Authors**: Historians of medieval South and Central Asia.
   - **Details**: Analyzes medieval historical texts referencing Afghan and Pashtun-related communities, emphasizing cautious interpretation of early ethnonyms.
   - **Claim Supported**: Historical documentation and evolution of early references.

3. **Encyclopædia Britannica & Academic Ethnography**: *Genealogical Traditions and the Israelite Origin Theory*.
   - **Authors**: Anthropologists and historians of Afghan tribes.
   - **Details**: Examines traditional genealogical frameworks, including the Qais Abdur Rashid and Israelite-origin narratives, interpreting them as culturally vital genealogical traditions rather than verified biological history.
   - **Claim Supported**: Nature of Pashtun genealogical and origin traditions.

4. **Peer-Reviewed Historical Scholarship**: *Ethnogenesis and Population Dynamics*.
   - **Authors**: Scholars of Central Asian ethnohistory.
   - **Details**: Explores the multi-centennial process of ethnogenesis involving migration, linguistic adaptation, tribal confederations, and intermixing.
   - **Claim Supported**: Complex historical formation of the Pashtun people.
