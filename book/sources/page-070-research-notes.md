# Research Notes: Page 70 — Religion & Community Life

## Core Concept
- **Religion as Practice**: In Orakzai society, Islam is not just a personal belief system but a collective practice that shapes family life, community interactions, and social responsibility.
- **Institutions of Faith**: The Mosque (*Masjid*), the Madrasa, and the role of the Ulema (religious scholars) are central to the social and educational landscape.
- **Intersection with Culture**: Religious practices like Ramadan, Eid, and Zakat intersect with Pashtun values of hospitality and community support.

## Key Findings

### 1. Religious Education and Madaris
- **Moral Development**: Parents in Orakzai view *Deeni Madaris* (religious schools) as essential for the spiritual and moral development of their children, teaching not just literacy in the Qur'an but also social ethics.
- **Source**: *Parental Perceptions of Disciplinary Practices in Deeni Madaris: A Qualitative Study from District Orakzai* (2026).

### 2. Social Cohesion and Local Supporters
- **Religious Figures**: Imams and religious scholars are identified as "local supporters" who play a significant role in promoting social cohesion and stability in the district.
- **Source**: *The role of local supporters in promoting social cohesion in District Orakzai* (2024).

### 3. Sectarian Diversity and Sufi Influence
- **Sufi Lineage**: There is documented history of Sufi lines of thought in Orakzai, contributing to the spiritual diversity of the region.
- **Source**: *Editing Sufism: Contemporary Negotiations* (Brill, 2023).

### 4. Community Practice: Ramadan and Zakat
- **Collective Responsibility**: Ramadan is a peak period for community support, with Zakat and Sadaqah serving as vital tools for social security and helping the less fortunate.
- **Source**: *A Comprehensive Review on Zakāt's Role in Framing Social Security* (2020).

### 5. The Mosque as a Social Hub
- **Congregational Prayer**: Friday prayers (*Jumu'ah*) serve as the primary weekly gathering point for the male community, where social announcements and collective decisions are often shared.
- **Source**: General ethnographic records of the Merged Districts.

## Evidence Hierarchy
- **A. Orakzai-Specific**: 2026 study on Madaris in Orakzai; 2024 study on social cohesion in Orakzai; Saira B. Orakzai's research.
- **B. Pashtun-Wide**: Hujra vs. Mosque dynamics; Pashtun religious identity research.
- **C. Islamic**: The Five Pillars; Qur'anic ethics; Zakat requirements.
- **D. Contemporary**: Digital religious learning; modern madrasa reforms.

## Documented Terminology
- **Masjid**: Mosque (Place of worship and community gathering).
- **Madrasa**: Religious school (Center for formal Islamic education).
- **Ulema**: Religious scholars (Authorities on faith and law).
- **Zakat**: Obligatory charity (Tool for community support).
- **Ramadan / Eid**: Sacred month and festivals (Core periods of communal practice).
- **Hifz**: Memorization of the Qur'an (A key educational goal).
