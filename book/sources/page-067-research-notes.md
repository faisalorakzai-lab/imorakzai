# Research Notes: Page 67 — The Role of Women

## Core Concept
- **Women as Cultural Carriers**: Women in Orakzai society are the primary transmitters of language, family memory, and social values.
- **Agency and Resilience**: Beyond traditional roles, women contribute to conflict prevention, agriculture, and increasingly, digital and professional life.
- **Evidence-Based Approach**: Distinguishing between Islamic principles, Pashtun-wide customs, and Orakzai-specific documented practices.

## Key Findings

### 1. Social Role and Conflict Prevention
- **Early Warning Systems**: Tribal women in Orakzai and Kurram act as informal "early warning" sensors, identifying community tensions and issuing warnings to elders to prevent conflict.
- **Source**: *Early Warning by Women for Conflict Prevention in the Erstwhile FATA* (Noreen Naseer, 2026).

### 2. Proverbial Identity and Globalization
- **Proverbs about Women**: A study on Orakzai cultural values shows that proverbs are key tools for defining women's roles, though these values are being reinterpreted by younger generations due to globalization.
- **Source**: *Globalization's Influence on Pashtun Cultural Values: A Study of Proverbs about Women* (M. A. Orakzai, 2025).

### 3. Education and Literacy
- **Literacy Rates**: Female literacy in Orakzai remains significantly low, reported below 30% in the 2023 Census, highlighting a major gender gap compared to urban centers.
- **Source**: *Literacy Between the 2017 and 2023 Censuses* (Gallup Pakistan/PBS, 2023).

### 4. Health and Maternal Care
- **Maternal Mortality**: Sociocultural practices, including restrictions on visiting hospitals without male partners, significantly impact maternal health and mortality rates in the Orakzai district.
- **Source**: *Nexus between sociocultural practices and maternal mortality in Pakistan* (M. R. Sheikh, 2026).

### 5. Agricultural and Economic Contribution
- **Rural Economy**: Women are essential to the agricultural workforce, managing livestock, food processing, and household gardening, though their contributions are often underreported in formal economic data.
- **Source**: *Women Farmers' Access to and Control Over Farming Resources in KP* (M. Safdar, 2021).

## Evidence Hierarchy
- **A. Orakzai-Specific**: Noreen Naseer's 2026 study on conflict prevention; M. A. Orakzai's 2025 study on proverbs; 2023 Census data.
- **B. Pashtun-Wide**: General roles in language transmission and family structure (*Kor*).
- **C. Regional**: Agricultural roles and health disparities across the Merged Districts (erstwhile FATA).
- **D. Contemporary**: Digital participation via smartphones and social media in urbanized families.

## Documented Terminology
- **Kor**: Household / Home (the primary domain of female authority).
- **Mataloona**: Proverbs (often used to transmit social expectations).
- **Melmastia**: Hospitality (a collective family practice involving women's labor).
- **Nang**: Honor (conceptually linked to the protection and dignity of women).
- **Mashara**: Elder woman / Matriarch.
