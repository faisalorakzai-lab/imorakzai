# Research Notes: Page 92 — Orakzai in Karachi

## Core Concept
- **The Urban Journey**: Orakzai migration to Karachi is part of a larger historical movement of Pashtuns from the northwest to the southern port city.
- **Beyond the Mountains**: Identity is not tied solely to geography; it is carried, adapted, and maintained in the urban environment.
- **Evidence Distinction**: Critical to distinguish between broader Pashtun migration patterns and specific Orakzai evidence.

## Karachi as a Migration Destination
- **Diverse Metropolis**: Karachi is home to Urdu-speaking, Sindhi, Pashtun, Punjabi, Baloch, and other communities.
- **Pashtun Hub**: Pashtuns are documented as the largest internal migrant community in Karachi (IGC, 2024).
- **Historical Waves**:
  - **1950s–60s**: Industrial growth and construction boom attracted labor from the northwest.
  - **1970s–80s**: Expansion into transport and retail sectors.
  - **2000s–Present**: Conflict-induced displacement and search for better services (education, health).

## Orakzai-Specific Presence
- **Neighborhoods**: Orakzai families are found within larger Pashtun concentrations in:
  - **District West**: Areas like Banaras, Pathan Colony, and Orangi.
  - **District Malir/Gadap**: Sohrab Goth and surrounding areas.
  - **Keamari**: Proximity to transport hubs.
- **Occupations**:
  - **Transport**: Heavy involvement in the trucking and public transport industry.
  - **Retail/Trade**: Small businesses, cloth markets, and general stores.
  - **Professional**: Growing number of students and professionals in medicine, engineering, and government service.
- **Chain Migration**: Documented pattern where a "pioneer" migrant (often a male family member) establishes a foothold, followed by relatives and eventually the relocation of entire households.

## Identity and Adaptation
- **Trans-local Identity**: Maintaining a "memory of return" to the Orakzai village while navigating the complexities of Karachi life.
- **Language**: Pashto remains the primary language of the home and community spaces (Hujras in the city).
- **Social Networks**: Tribal and clan-based associations provide support for new arrivals (housing, jobs).
- **Synthesis**: Creating a "home in the city" through food, religious practices, and social gatherings.

## Research Gaps
- **Population Data**: No official census provides a breakdown of Pashtun sub-tribes (like Orakzai) in Karachi. Figures are based on community estimates and localized academic fieldwork.
- **Systematic Mapping**: Lack of published maps specifically delineating "Orakzai-majority" streets, as settlement is often mixed with other Pashtun tribes (Afridi, Mehsud, etc.).
