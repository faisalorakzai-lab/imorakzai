# Research Notes: Page 103 — Preserving Culture in a Digital World

This document records the research findings and methodological decisions for **Page 103: Preserving Culture in a Digital World**.

## 1. Digital Preservation Frameworks

### UNESCO Guidelines
- **Source:** *Guidelines for the Preservation of Digital Heritage* (2003) and *UNESCO/PERSIST Guidelines* (2nd Ed, 2021).
- **Key Principles:** Selection for long-term preservation, metadata standards, format management, and the distinction between digitization and preservation.
- **Intangible Heritage Domains:** Oral traditions, performing arts, social practices, rituals, festive events, knowledge concerning nature, and traditional craftsmanship.

### Digital Preservation Coalition (DPC)
- **Concept:** Digital preservation is a series of managed activities necessary to ensure continued access to digital materials for as long as necessary.
- **Risks:** Media obsolescence, format obsolescence, loss of metadata, and lack of institutional/community stewardship.

## 2. Pakistan & Pashto Specific Research

### Language Revitalization in Pakistan
- **Source:** Mehmood et al. (2025), *Integrating Digital Tools for the Documentation and Revitalization of Minority Languages in Pakistan*.
- **Tools Identified:** FLEx (FieldWorks Language Explorer), ELAN (EUDICO Linguistic Annotator), Duolingo, Memrise.
- **Case Studies:** Torwali language revitalization (Manan, 2017) shows the importance of identity-based integrated approaches.
- **Pashto Context:** Rahman (2020) discusses the role of digital archives in preserving Pashto.

### Institutional Evidence
- **Lok Virsa (National Institute of Folk and Traditional Heritage):** Acts as the primary repository for intangible heritage in Pakistan.
- **KP Culture and Tourism Authority:** Documents regional festivals and crafts, though specific Orakzai digital archive evidence remains limited.

## 3. Oral History Methodology

### Oral History in the Digital Age (OHDA)
- **Source:** Boyd (2012), *Search, Explore, Connect*.
- **Workflow:** Consent → Preparation → Recording → Identification → Transcription → Translation → Contextualization → Storage → Backup → Preservation.
- **Ethics:** Informed consent must cover intended use, access levels (Open/Limited/Restricted/Private), and the right to withdraw.

## 4. Evidence Boundaries & Cautions

- **Archive vs. Social Media:** Social media is for distribution; an archive is for preservation. Do not equate a Facebook group with a digital archive.
- **Orakzai-Specific Data:** While Pashto digital resources exist, Orakzai-specific digital heritage documentation is a significant research gap.
- **AI Ethics:** AI is a tool for assistance (OCR, transcription), not a source of historical truth. Fabricated AI history is a major risk.
- **Genealogy:** Oral genealogy is evidence of tradition, not necessarily verified biological fact.

## 5. Metadata Standards

- **Dublin Core Metadata Initiative (DCMI):** Standard fields include Title, Creator, Subject, Description, Publisher, Contributor, Date, Type, Format, Identifier, Source, Language, Relation, Coverage, and Rights.
- **Contextual Metadata:** For Orakzai records, metadata should include location (village/valley), speaker identity (with consent), and cultural context.
