# Research Notes: Page 119 — Cloud Computing

This document records the research findings and methodological decisions for **Page 119: Cloud Computing**.

## 1. Technical Framework (NIST Standards)
- **Definition:** A model for enabling ubiquitous, convenient, on-demand network access to a shared pool of configurable computing resources (e.g., networks, servers, storage, applications, and services).
- **Five Essential Characteristics:**
    1. On-demand self-service.
    2. Broad network access.
    3. Resource pooling.
    4. Rapid elasticity.
    5. Measured service.
- **Service Models:**
    - **IaaS (Infrastructure as a Service):** Compute, storage, and networking.
    - **PaaS (Platform as a Service):** Development and deployment environments.
    - **SaaS (Software as a Service):** Application delivery over the web.
- **Deployment Models:** Public, Private, Hybrid, and Community cloud.

## 2. Pakistan Cloud Context (2025–2026)
- **Cloud First Policy (2022):** Mandates federal public sector entities to prioritize cloud solutions for new IT investments to reduce costs and encourage investment.
- **National Data Governance Policy 2026:** Finalized in June 2026; establishes a framework for government data management and mandates that sensitive national data must be stored on physical servers within Pakistan (Data Residency).
- **Market Dynamics:** The Pakistan public cloud market is estimated at ~US$332 million in 2025, with a growing CAGR driven by AI adoption and digital stability.
- **Institutional Role:** The Ministry of IT & Telecommunication (MoITT) and the National Information Technology Board (NITB) lead cloud accreditation and infrastructure initiatives.

## 3. Cloud & Emerging Technologies
- **AI Infrastructure:** Cloud platforms provide access to GPUs and specialized compute for AI workloads.
- **Edge Computing:** Workloads are distributed closer to the user to reduce latency, complementing cloud systems.
- **Fintech & E-commerce:** Cloud-native architectures support Raast integration, digital banking, and scalable online marketplaces.

## 4. Case Study: Faisal Orakzai — Remote-First Systems
- **Perspective:** Building digital systems (software, blockchain, platforms) using cloud-native and remote-first methodologies.
- **Significance:** Illustrates how individuals from remote regions like Orakzai can access global-scale computing power without owning physical hardware.
- **Fact vs. Interpretation:** Biographical details are restricted to documented associations (OkzByte Hub, Orakzai Group), while the "Systems Philosophy" is presented as an entrepreneurial perspective.

## 5. Structural Challenges & Risks
- **Data Residency:** Balancing global provider benefits with national data sovereignty requirements.
- **Cloud Lock-in:** Risks associated with proprietary APIs and data formats.
- **Infrastructure Gap:** The dependency of "cloud" on physical data centers, power, and cooling, which remains a challenge in remote areas.
- **Security:** The "Shared Responsibility Model" between providers and customers.
