# Sources: Page 85 — Orakzai Agency

This document records the constitutional, administrative, and academic sources supporting **Page 85: Orakzai Agency** in *I’M ORAKZAI*.

## Constitutional & Legal Primary Sources

1. **Government of Pakistan.** (1973). *The Constitution of the Islamic Republic of Pakistan*.
   - **Articles**: 246 (Definition of Tribal Areas), 247 (Administration of Tribal Areas).
   - **Claim Supported**: Orakzai Agency's constitutional status within FATA.

2. **National Assembly of Pakistan.** (2018). *The Constitution (Twenty-fifth Amendment) Act, 2018*.
   - **Details**: Integration of FATA into KP and repeal of Article 247.
   - **Claim Supported**: Transition from Orakzai Agency to Orakzai District.

3. **Frontier Crimes Regulation (FCR).** (1901).
   - **Details**: The legal code governing the tribal areas until 2018.
   - **Claim Supported**: Legal framework of collective responsibility and Political Agent authority.

## Administrative & Statistical Records

4. **Pakistan Bureau of Statistics (PBS).** (2017). *Final Results of 6th Population & Housing Census-2017: Orakzai Agency*.
   - **Data**: Population (254,303), Area (1,538 km²), Urban/Rural split.
   - **Claim Supported**: 2017 Census snapshot for the Agency.

5. **Government of Pakistan.** (1973). *Gazette of Pakistan (Nov 3, 1973)*.
   - **Details**: Notification of the creation of Orakzai Agency.
   - **Claim Supported**: Establishment date (Announcement).

6. **District Courts Orakzai.** (2024). *Historical Overview of Orakzai*.
   - **Details**: History of transition from FR Kohat to Agency on Dec 1, 1973.
   - **Claim Supported**: Inauguration date and historical administrative background.

## Academic & Scholarly Research

7. **Ahmed, Akbar S.** (1980). *Pukhtun Economy and Society: Traditional Structure and Economic Development in a Tribal Society*. Routledge.
   - **Details**: Ethnographic study of the Orakzai Agency during the 1970s.
   - **Claim Supported**: Tribal structure, role of elders, and early Agency administration.

8. **Ullah, A.** (2009). "The Governance of Federally Administered Tribal Areas under FCR." *National Institute of Historical and Cultural Research*.
   - **Details**: Analysis of the Political Agent's role and the FCR mechanism.
   - **Claim Supported**: Administrative hierarchy and governance model.
