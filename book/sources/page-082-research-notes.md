# Research Notes: Page 82 — 1947 & the New Pakistan

## Core Concept
- **State-Building vs. Country-Formation**: Distinguishing between the event of independence (1947) and the long-term process of building state institutions (administration, law, citizenship).
- **Orakzai's Administrative Path**: Orakzai was part of **Frontier Region (FR) Kohat** in 1947. It functioned as a "tribal area" adjacent to the settled district of Kohat, rather than an independent Agency (which was only established in 1973).
- **The Negotiated State**: Early Pakistan's relationship with the frontier was defined by negotiation, maintaining colonial structures (FCR, Political Agents) while introducing new national sovereignty.

## Key Findings

### 1. The Early Administrative Structure (1947-1955)
- **Inherited Institutions**: Pakistan maintained the Political Agency system. For Orakzai, this meant continuing the relationship with the administration in Kohat.
- **Operation Curzon (1947)**: Jinnah ordered the withdrawal of regular army troops from the tribal areas by December 1947. This was a "trust-building" measure, leaving security to local militias (Scouts) and tribal lashkars.
- **Source**: *The Pashtun Borderlands* (EUP, 2021); *Defence Journal* (2018).

### 2. Constitutional Development
- **Objectives Resolution (1949)**: Established the foundational principles of the Pakistani state—Islamic, democratic, and federal. It provided the ideological framework for national identity that would gradually reach the frontier.
- **One Unit Scheme (1955)**: The merger of West Pakistan's provinces into a single unit. While NWFP was merged, the **Federally Administered Tribal Areas (FATA)** maintained their special status under the central government.
- **Source**: *Constitutionalizing a perpetual transition* (Cheema & Yousaf, 2020); *Wikipedia*.

### 3. Orakzai and FR Kohat
- **Status**: Orakzai territory was administratively linked to Kohat district as a "Frontier Region." It was not a separate "Agency" in the early Pakistan period.
- **Transition**: The Political Agent of the time (often the Deputy Commissioner of Kohat acting in a dual role for the FR) managed the relationship through Maliks and Jirgas.
- **Source**: *Orakzai District* (Wikipedia); *About Orakzai Courts*.

### 4. Continuity and Change
- **Continuity**: The **Frontier Crimes Regulation (FCR)** remained the primary legal framework. Tribal leadership (Maliks) continued as intermediaries.
- **Change**: New symbols of sovereignty (flag, national anthem, Pakistani identity documents) began to enter public life. The withdrawal of troops marked a shift from a "military frontier" to a "political frontier."
- **Source**: *Tribal elders and societal inequities* (Haroon, 2022).

### 5. Social and Economic Integration
- **Markets**: Orakzai communities remained deeply connected to the markets of **Kohat** and **Hangu**.
- **Education**: Formal schooling began to expand slowly, with the first government schools in the frontier regions appearing in the late 1940s and early 1950s.
- **Source**: *Mapping Histories* (Tandfonline, 2024).

## Author's Reflection Themes
- **The Process of Belonging**: Citizenship is not just a legal status but a gradual social and political integration.
- **Stability through Continuity**: The state used old tools (FCR, Maliks) to manage a new reality, creating a "perpetual transition."
- **Resilience**: Orakzai society maintained its internal structures while adapting to the new national framework.
